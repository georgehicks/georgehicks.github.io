BANDO JOURNEY

What Do You Really Want?

Jesus surfaces the true desire. That desire is for completeness — wholeness, soundness, the perfection of the Father’s love. Abiding in the Son both shapes the want and opens the way to receive it.

Click any reference for the full Berean text. Gold words inside the text open the Greek or Hebrew. Collapsible stops hold optional depth, especially the final act.

1

Jesus Surfaces the Want

He does not assume. He asks so the real desire can be named and brought to Him.

“What do you want?”John 1:38
“Do you want to get well?”John 5:6 — well = sound / whole (ὑγιής)
“What do you want Me to do for you?”Mark 10:51

The disciples answer by asking where He abides. Desire is meant to become remaining.

2

The Want Is for Completeness

Not a better circumstance first. Completeness, maturity, the end for which we exist — love that is whole like the Father’s.

“Be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect.”Matt 5:48

τέλειος — complete, mature, whole. Context is love of enemies (Matt 5:43–48). Parallel: be merciful as the Father is merciful (Luke 6:36).

Same word family in Scripture
  • James 1:4 — mature and complete, lacking nothing.
  • Eph 4:13 — to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ.
  • 1 John 4:12, 17–18 — love made complete; perfect love casts out fear.
  • Col 2:10 — in Him you have been made complete.
3

The Lacking to Completeness

We often answer Jesus’ question with a condition: if only this were better, then I would be whole. That is the counterfeit path. There are also real lacks that are good to name — they still do not complete us.

“What do I still lack?” — then he went away sorrowful.Mark 10:17–22
“If you knew the gift of God… you would have asked Him…”John 4:10
Common counterfeits and legitimate lackings

Common Counterfeits

Things we quietly believe would complete us.

  • If only this relationship were right…
  • If only I had the position, recognition, success…
  • If only my body / health were fixed…
  • If only I had enough money or security…
  • If only I were understood or approved…
  • If only the past were different…
  • If only I felt peaceful / confident / free…

Common Legitimate Lackings

Real needs. Good to name. Still not the source of completeness.

  • Rest and margin
  • Healing in body or relationships
  • Daily provision and work that matters
  • True friendship and belonging
  • Clarity about the next step
  • Freedom from a particular fear or habit
  • Strength to keep going when the load is heavy
4

Asking That Flows from Remaining

The promise is real. The condition is remaining in the Son so His words form the wish. Completeness is received, not assembled.

“If you remain in Me and My words remain in you, ask whatever you wish, and it will be done for you.”John 15:7

Even Jesus had a want. In Gethsemane He said, “not as I will, but as You will.” The same word — θέλω. He brought His desire to the Father as surrender, not suppression. The want is real; the final word belongs to the Father.

“Yet not as I will, but as You will.”Matt 26:39
  • John 14:13–14; John 16:23–24 — ask in My name… that your joy may be complete.
  • Matt 7:7–11 — Ask, seek, knock. The Father gives good gifts.
  • Matt 11:28–30 — Take My yoke… and you will find rest for your souls.

Abiding is the connected way of life with Father, Son, and Spirit. The life of the Vine produces the fruit of completeness.

5

Final Act — If There Is Time

Deep dive into prayerful asking. Not a polished answer. The real want, brought to the Son.

Prayerful asking: What do I really want?

Stay with Jesus long enough for His words to touch the want. Then ask.

  • Name the actual want (not the polished version or the “if only”).
  • Bring it to Him: “Jesus, this is what I want…”
  • Listen. What does He say? What does He show?
  • Ask from the place of remaining, not from the place of lack that must be fixed first.
  • Do the next thing He gives — like Bartimaeus, follow on the road.
Jesus is the way, the truth, and the life. Abiding in Him is remaining in the Vine so the life of the Vine flows.John 14:6 · John 15

If time is short, this stop can be closed and the journey ends with the asking that flows from remaining.

Surprising Connections

Desire for completeness points forward to Christ — the One who satisfies every true thirst and finishes the work.

Well = Whole

John 5:6 — ὑγιής. Jesus asks if the man wants to be made sound/whole, not merely fixed at the pool.

It Is Finished

John 19:30 — τετέλεσται. Same root as τέλειος. The work of completeness is already done in the Son.

Come, Everyone Who Thirsts

Isa 55:1 → John 4, John 7. The invitation is into the everlasting covenant (Isa 55:3).

Delight First

Psalm 37:4 — delight in the LORD and He gives the desires of the heart. The delight reshapes the want.

The Shared Yoke

Matt 11:28–30 — ζυγός. Taking His yoke is remaining connected so the soul finds rest.

Perfect Love

1 John 4:18 — perfect love casts out the fear that we are not yet whole.
