BANDO JOURNEY

Completeness vs. “If Only”

Be perfect as your heavenly Father is perfect. Completeness is not assembled from improved circumstances. It is received by remaining in the Son.

Click any reference for the full Berean text. Gold underlined words open an inline card with the key Greek or Hebrew.

1

The “If Only” That Promises Wholeness

Jesus surfaces the true want. Many of us answer with a condition: if only this person, circumstance, or achievement were better, then I would be whole.

“What do you want?”John 1:38
“Do you want to get well?”John 5:6

The “if only” treats something in the fallen world as the missing piece. Scripture shows this path does not complete us.

2

What “Perfect” Means

Not sinless performance. Completeness, maturity, the end for which we exist — love that is whole like the Father’s.

“Be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect.”Matt 5:48

Context is love of enemies (Matt 5:43–48). Parallel: “Be merciful, just as your Father is merciful” (Luke 6:36).

Same Word Family

  • James 1:4 — that you may be mature and complete, lacking nothing.
  • Eph 4:13 — to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ.
  • 1 John 4:12, 17–18 — love made complete; perfect love casts out fear.
3

The Counterfeit Path Named

Those who treat something else as the completing piece walk away or remain unsatisfied.

“What do I still lack?” — then he went away sorrowful.Mark 10:17–22
“If you knew the gift of God… you would have asked Him, and He would have given you living water.”John 4:10

Bartimaeus names the real want and follows (Mark 10:51). The rich man does not.

3b

Naming What We Treat as the Missing Piece

Two lists. Not to analyze, but to speak. What is the actual “if only” for you right now? And what is a real lack that is still not the thing that makes you whole?

Common Counterfeits

Things we quietly believe would complete us if they were finally better.

  • If only this relationship were right…
  • If only I had the position, the recognition, the success…
  • If only my body / health were fixed…
  • If only I had enough money or security…
  • If only I were understood or approved…
  • If only the past were different…
  • If only I felt peaceful / confident / free…

Common Legitimate Lackings

Real needs. Good to name. Still not the source of completeness.

  • Rest and margin
  • Healing in body or relationships
  • Daily provision and work that matters
  • True friendship and belonging
  • Clarity about the next step
  • Freedom from a particular fear or habit
  • Strength to keep going when the load is heavy

Jesus already asked the question. The invitation is to answer it out loud: “What do you really want?” Then stay with Him long enough for His words to touch the want.

4

Completeness Already in the Son

Wholeness is received, not assembled. It is the life of the Vine flowing because we remain.

“In Him you have been made complete.”Col 2:10
“It is finished.”John 19:30
“If you remain in Me and My words remain in you, ask whatever you wish…”John 15:7

Abiding is the connected way of life with Father, Son, and Spirit. The desire itself is reshaped as His words abide in us.

5

In This Fallen World

Completeness does not wait for every “if only” to be resolved. It is available now by coming to the Son and remaining in Him while the Kingdom grows here.

  • Name the actual want (not the polished “if only”).
  • Bring it to Jesus and stay long enough for His words to touch it.
  • Ask from the place of remaining, not from the place of lack that must be fixed first.
  • Do the next thing He gives — like Bartimaeus, follow on the road.
Jesus is the way, the truth, and the life. Abiding in Him is remaining in the Vine so the life of the Vine flows.John 14:6 · John 15
✦

Surprising Connections

Completeness in Scripture points forward to Christ — the One who satisfies every true thirst and finishes the work.

Thirst Answered by the Struck Son

Ex 17 / Num 20 — the rock yields water. 1 Cor 10:4 “that Rock was Christ.” Wholeness is not a better well; it is the Son.

Come, Everyone Who Thirsts

Isa 55:1 → John 4, John 7:37–38. The invitation is into the everlasting covenant (Isa 55:3).

Delight First

Psalm 37:4 — delight in the LORD and He gives the desires of the heart. The delight reshapes the want.

Perfect Love Casts Out Fear

1 John 4:18 — the fear that we are not yet whole is driven out as the Father’s love is known and remains.

Come to Me

Matt 11:28–30 — rest for the soul is already offered. Completeness does not wait for the load to disappear first.

It Is Finished

John 19:30 — τετέλεσται. The same root as τέλειος. The work of completeness is already accomplished in the Son.
