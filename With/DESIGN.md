# With (Immanuel Prayer) — Design

Status: draft for review. Nothing is built yet. Restructured around practice-first, with two support frames: **What's in the way?** (answering objections) and **Why practice hearing from God?**

## Purpose

A small PWA that helps a person hear from God. It is training wheels for listening prayer: get still and turn toward Christ, bring one real thing to Him, ask a simple question, write down whatever comes, test it, and keep it.

The app is primarily practice. Newcomers and seasoned people use the same experience: a calm start, a guided form that coaches the first few times and then gets out of the way. Everything else in the app exists to support practice and to clear the way for it. Two support frames do that: **What's in the way?** (answering objections honestly) and **Why practice hearing from God?** (reasons this is worth doing).

It is a guided form, not a teacher and not a voice. The app asks the questions. The person asks God and writes the answers. The app never composes words for God.

## Inspired by, not a copy

This app is inspired by the work of Jamie and Donna Winship, Karl and Charlotte Lehman, Jim Wilder and colleagues, Charles Kraft, John Eldredge, Mark Virkler and Dallas Willard. It borrows the shape of their practices and is free to adapt, combine, shorten or leave out parts. It does not reproduce their methods, claim to teach them as they teach them, or speak for them. None of them has endorsed or reviewed it.

Consequences:
- Path names credit the inspiration ("inspired by Winship") but wording is our own. No long quotations from their books.
- Where our version departs from a teacher's, we don't pretend otherwise. The References page sends the person to the originals for the full teaching.
- We shape each step for newcomers first, even where that means it no longer matches the source exactly.
- Wording is accountable to Scripture, to pastoral review and to the safety rules below, not to a teacher's text.

## Principles

1. **Scripture first.** Hearing God is not exotic. It is what Jesus said His sheep do. The app keeps showing that.
2. **Practice first.** The first screen is the breath. One tap to begin, one more to the first question. No quiz, no sign-up, no reading required.
3. **One experience for everyone.** Training wheels fade by use, not by a choice made on day one.
4. **Connection before content.** Stillness and turning toward Him come before asking anything.
5. **No invented words from God.** No AI, no generated "responses." The person types or dictates their own.
6. **Private by default.** Everything lives on the device. No accounts, no network calls beyond loading the app. Scripture text is in the app, so nothing links out except the book links on References.
7. **A safe way out.** If a path gets heavy, the app sends the person back to something glad and points to human help when needed.
8. **Matches IAmHere.** Same stack, same look, same breathing component, same conventions.

## Our position, and the support layer

### The position

The app is not neutral. It holds three convictions and says them plainly and kindly:

1. **It is scriptural.** That God speaks to His people, that they can hear Him, and that they are expected to is the plain teaching of Scripture, from Genesis to Revelation. The app makes that case from the text.
2. **The church has long believed it.** Respected Christians of every age, from the second century to now, have taught and practiced listening to God. They are named, dated and quoted.
3. **It is the life Jesus wants for us.** Interactive faith is not a niche practice. It is what He offered: friends, not servants. The app shows what that looks like in ordinary days.

What honesty requires of us:
- **State the objection at its strongest.** No straw men. "Isn't the Bible enough?" and "Doesn't prophecy cease?" are real questions asked by serious Christians.
- **Say where Christians disagree, once, and then make the case.** One plain line, no hedging in every answer.
- **Separate the principle from the forms.** Scripture teaches that God speaks and that we are to listen, test and obey. It does not prescribe breathing exercises, glad memories, or a seven-step journal. Those are *wise helps* drawn from teachers, and the app says so. The principle is entirely scriptural. The helps are tools that serve it.
- **Be careful with witnesses.** Quotes show the practice is old and tested, not that each person would endorse this app, or each other. Context is noted where a quote could be misread.
- **Never claim more than Scripture does.** Hearing God never adds doctrine, never outranks Scripture, and never exempts a person from testing.
- **No pressure.** The person's own conclusion matters more than ours. The *Why practice?* side invites. It does not corner.

### The support layer: two frames

Practice is the app. These two areas serve it. They are two sides of one place, with a toggle at the top that opens on *What's in the way?*, since doubt usually comes before reasons. Full titles on the pages, short labels (**What's in the way?** and **Why practice?**) on the toggle and the tab.

- **What's in the way?** Honest answers to the doubts that keep people from starting or make them stop. Each card ends with a matching *Why practice?* piece and a **Try it** button that goes back to practice.
- **Why practice hearing from God?** Reasons this is worth doing: the Scripture case, the witnesses of the ages, and what the life looks like. Positive, short, skippable.

How it works:
- **Never a prerequisite.** No one has to read anything to begin. The first screen is practice.
- **Surfaced at the point of doubt.** On any practice step a small **Not sure?** chip opens the relevant *What's in the way?* card as a bottom sheet. Closing it returns the person to the same step with their draft intact. This keeps practice screens clean.
- **Also browsable.** The same cards live in the *Why practice?* / *What's in the way?* area for anyone who wants to read first.
- **Each card is short and stands alone.** A person can leave at any point and lose nothing.
- **Invitation, not argument.** We are showing a door, not winning a debate. Every card ends with the same low-stakes offer: *Want to try one question?*

## Audience and friction

The people this app is for do not know how to do this. Many are not sure it is allowed, or real, or safe. The design goal is that nothing in the first five minutes gives them a reason to close the app.

### Who is arriving

| Person | What they are thinking | What they need first |
|---|---|---|
| **The believer who thinks it's for the elite** | "That's for pastors and mystics, not me." | To see that Jesus said His sheep hear Him, plainly and for everyone. |
| **The afraid-of-deception believer** | "What if I'm hearing myself, or something worse?" | A clear test, a safe way to stop, and a promise nothing is forced. |
| **The Scripture-sufficiency believer** | "God has spoken in the Bible. Isn't that enough?" | Respect, not argument. Proof that this practice stays under Scripture. |
| **The one who got silence** | "I tried. Nothing." | Permission for silence, and smaller, easier things to try. |
| **The one who was burned** | "A leader told me God said something to me." | No leader, nothing to join, and all authority staying with them. |
| **The one wary of imagination** | "Isn't this New Age visualization?" | Plain language, no required imagining, and a straight answer. |
| **The seeker or doubter** | "I'm not sure anyone is there." | No pressure, honest welcome, a low-stakes first try. |
| **The wounded** | "I want healing but I'm fragile." | Safety first, a glad place, and a way out at every step. |

### What the research says

- **Believing God wants to speak is itself a barrier.** Teachers on this topic say many Christians doubt that God speaks to them individually, assume it is for the spiritually elite, or write off subtle impressions as their own imagination. ([Building Jerusalem](https://buildingjerusalem.blog/2017/08/29/listening-to-gods-voice/), [prayer-coach.com](https://prayer-coach.com/hear-the-voice-of-god/))
- **Expectation is the first problem.** His voice rarely arrives as a thunderclap. It comes as a thought, a picture, a verse that glows, or a persistent conviction. People expecting something dramatic conclude nothing happened. ([GotQuestions](https://www.gotquestions.org/hearing-God.html))
- **Gentle words get doubted.** People expect correction, so when what comes is tender they distrust it. ([Blessed Transgressions](https://blessedtransgressions.org/eight-hindrances-gods-voice/))
- **Fear of deception is real and common.** The usual answer is to stay close to the Word, compare what comes against it, and let time in His presence train the ear. The shepherd-and-sheep picture of John 10 is the standard way to explain it to beginners. ([Ellel](https://ellel.org/au/daily-devotionals/sheep-i), [World Challenge](https://worldchallenge.org/devotions?page=239))
- **There is a real theological objection.** Some Christians hold that God's direct speech ended with Scripture. Willard answers them in *Hearing God*, but his critics say the biblical case for treating this as a normal part of every Christian's life is weak, and that it can make people feel defective if they don't hear. ([Pneuma Review](https://pneumareview.com/?p=23481), [Spirituality & Practice](https://www.spiritualityandpractice.com/book-reviews/view/23631/hearing-god)) The app must not imply that a person is behind or failing.
- **Inner-healing methods draw criticism.** Critics raise visualization and imagination, New Age origins, the risk of false or fabricated memories, and whether Christ is "in the room" after His ascension. ([Christian Answers for the New Age](https://www.christiananswersnewage.com/article/the-immanuel-approach-prayer-and-the-contemplative-invasion), [Inner Healing Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Healing_Movement)) Defenders say the method is orthodox. ([Lehman notes on Catholic orthodoxy](https://www.immanuelapproach.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Catholic-orthodoxy-of-Immanuel.pdf)) The app should answer these honestly and let the person skip anything they aren't comfortable with.
- **Hearing voices has a mental-health side.** People who hear distressing or commanding voices need wise counsel and care. ([Christopher Cook](https://sanctuarymentalhealth.org/2020/06/14/chris-cook-christians-hearing-voices/)) The app must make this distinction explicit.
- **Prayer and meditation apps lose most people fast.** Across 93 mental-health apps the median 15-day retention was 3.9%. For prayer apps the onboarding itself is the friction: unfamiliar vocabulary, awkward silence, not knowing what comes next. Reminders set after a first session were reported to triple retention in Calm. ([Haifa study via UTHSCSA](https://scholars.uthscsa.edu/en/publications/mindfulness-meditation-app-abandonment-during-the-covid-19-pandem/), [Hallow review](https://yespress.io/hallow), [Amplitude on Calm](https://amplitude.com/blog/meditate-every-day))

Caveat: these sources are mostly popular teaching, blogs and secondary summaries. They are good for finding the real objections. They are not authority for the answers, which come from Scripture and should be reviewed by pastors.

### Friction points and how the design answers them

| Friction | Why it turns people off | Design response |
|---|---|---|
| **Unfamiliar vocabulary** ("inner healing," "Immanuel," "visualize," "regression") | Sounds like a program or like New Age | Plain words: *ask, notice, remember, write down what comes.* Explain "Immanuel" once ("God with us," Matthew 1:23), then use it sparingly. |
| **A wall of teaching before any experience** | Feels like homework | **Experience before explanation.** A first try is under two minutes. *What's in the way?* cards surface at the moment of doubt, not as a prerequisite. |
| **Fear it's not real or not safe** | Stops people before they start | A **Not sure?** chip on every practice step opens the matching card without leaving the step. The Test step and the stop button appear from the first session. |
| **Expecting a dramatic voice** | Quiet impressions are dismissed | An expectation line before the first ask: "Often it's a thought, a word, a verse, a picture, or a settled feeling. It can be small." A **See how He speaks** link sits beside it. |
| **Silence** | Reads as failure or rejection | A built-in "Nothing came?" step (below). Silence is a valid ending, never an error. |
| **Fear of making it up** | Paralysis | Say up front it may feel like your own thoughts, then give the Test and the idea of "write it down and see over time." |
| **Imagination and visualization** | Seen as New Age | Use *remember* not *picture*. Say "if you see something, write it; if you don't, that's fine." Every sensory prompt is skippable. |
| **"Jesus is in the room"** | Theological discomfort | Wording: "welcome Him," "ask where He was," with Matthew 28:20 and John 14:23. No instruction to invent or summon anything. |
| **Long forms** | Drop-off | Quick path under five minutes, a step counter, autosave, and "continue later" on every step. |
| **Writing is hard** | Blank box anxiety | Every box can be one word. Mic where supported. Optional sentence starters ("I think I heard...", "What stood out was..."). |
| **Guilt and pressure** | Makes it a performance | No streaks, scores, or "you missed a day." No claim that not hearing is a defect. |
| **Spiritual abuse history** | "Someone told me what God said" | State it: "No one else gets to tell you what God said. There's no leader here. You can stop any time." |
| **Doubt about God at all** | Feels excluded | A welcome for the unsure, framed as a low-stakes asking ("If You're there..." and Mark 9:24), with no pressure to believe. |
| **Heaviness in the Healing path** | Re-injury | Glad place gating, a stop-and-rest button, trained-person pointer. See Safety. |
| **Forgetting to come back** | Silent abandonment | After the first completed session, offer a one-tap reminder (`.ics`) once. No nagging. |

### One experience, training wheels that fade

There is no entry questionnaire. Everyone sees the same start screen: a **Begin** button that opens the breath and then the first question, with the other paths listed quietly below it.

Training wheels are *guidance* that appears on practice screens. It fades by use:

| Guidance | First sessions | After about three completed sessions |
|---|---|---|
| Expectation line before the first ask ("It's often small...") | Shown | Collapsed to a small "?" |
| Coaching lines under each prompt | Shown | Collapsed to a "?" on each step |
| Sentence starters in the boxes | Offered | Hidden, still one tap away |
| "It may sound like your own thoughts" note | Shown after each answer | Collapsed |
| **Not sure?** chip | Always visible | Always visible |
| **Nothing came?** link | Always visible | Always visible |
| Test and Keep steps | Always | Always |

Settings has a **Guidance** control: *Fade* (default), *Always show*, or *Hide*. A seasoned person can switch it to *Hide* straight away, and a newcomer never has to touch it.

Because seasoned and new users share the same flow, nothing is dumbed down and nothing is hidden. The difference is only how much coaching sits around the same steps.

### The "Nothing came?" step

Appears in every path wherever the person asks God something, as a quiet link under the box.

- "That's okay. Silence is allowed." (Psalm 62:1, 1 Kings 19:12) Optionally, C. S. Lewis on the same experience: "The silence is often so emphatic." (*Letters to Malcolm*, Letter 11). The point is that even a great Christian writer knew it.
- Three things to try, one at a time: wait one more breath; write what you *do* notice, even a blank, a word, a body feeling; ask Him "What is in the way?"
- A fourth: look at **How He speaks**. It may have come in a way you didn't count: a verse, a memory, a person, a calm, something that happened.
- A fifth: "Thank Him for something and come back later. Not hearing today doesn't mean He isn't speaking."
- Never ends in an error. The person can still finish, and the session saves with "nothing yet" as a normal answer.

### Language rules

- Second person, present tense, short lines.
- Prefer *ask*, *notice*, *remember*, *write down what comes*.
- Avoid *visualize*, *imagine Jesus*, *inner child*, *regression*, *download*, *channel*, *activate*, *unlock*, *manifest*.
- Avoid "God told me" in the app's own voice. The app says what the person *wrote*, never what God said.
- Do not use Christian-insider shorthand without a word of explanation the first time.
- Never shame, never promise results, never say what silence means.
- Say "may" and "often," not "will," "always" or "usually." God's habits are not ours to state.
- Name the people to test with in each tradition's words: pastor, priest, spiritual director, spiritual father, elder, or a mature friend.
- In cards about harm, speak plainly in the first person: "I'm sorry," not "we are sorry."
- Never use persistence over time as a test of whether something is from God.
- Never use 988 or crisis language inside a card. It appears only after a worrisome input.
- Speak safety in God's terms (Scripture, the Shepherd, the tests of the spirits, wise counsel), never in clinical or policy terms. No "prayer form," "trained person," "trauma work," "not counseling or medical care."

### Teaching placement

Not a lesson. A line, at the point of need.

| When | What appears |
|---|---|
| Before the first ask | The expectation line, plus John 10:27. |
| Under a box | A one-line prompt, and the **Not sure?** chip that opens the matching *What's in the way?* card as a sheet. |
| After an answer is written | "It may sound like your own thoughts. That's often how it comes. Write it down and test it." |
| At Test | The four questions, offered as three things to look at plus telling someone. |
| After Keep | The reminder offer, and "Come back and see how it holds up." |


## Research summary

The practices differ in vocabulary but share one spine:

1. Get still and turn toward Him (stillness, appreciation, asking).
2. Bring the real thing: one specific question, fear, or situation.
3. Ask, then receive: write down whatever comes, even if slight.
4. Test it: Scripture, Jesus, fruit.
5. Respond and keep it: one small step, and a record.

| Source | Shape | Role in this app |
|---|---|---|
| **Jamie and Donna Winship** | Bring it to God, then ask "What do You want me to know?" and "What do You want me to do?" | The default path (Quick). |
| **Karl and Charlotte Lehman, Immanuel Approach** | Glad memory, appreciation, welcome Jesus ("where are You?"), then bring the thing. The glad memory is the safety net. | The Deeper path. |
| **Jim Wilder, Anna Kang et al., Immanuel Journaling** | Seven steps starting with interactive gratitude, then a template modeled on God's words to Moses: I see you, I hear you, I understand, I am glad to be with you, I can do something about it. | The Journaling path. |
| **Charles Kraft, Deep Wounds, Deep Healing** | Ask the Holy Spirit to bring to mind the memory He wants to heal. Picture it, see that Jesus was there, give Him the hurt, forgive, let go of the lies, receive His truth. Keep asking and receiving until the healing comes. | The Healing path. Its repeat-until-healed shape becomes a return-as-often-as-needed loop. |
| **Mark Virkler, Four Keys** | Stillness, vision, spontaneity, two-way journaling. | Teaching behind Connected and the writing box. Not its own path. |
| **John Eldredge** | Questions put to God: what do You think of me, what do You want me to know, what do You want me to do. | Optional follow-up prompts after the Winship pair. |
| **Dallas Willard** | Hearing God as relationship, tested by Scripture and fruit. | Informs the Test step and *What's in the way?*. |

Sources used: [Winship episode](https://podcastaddict.com/episode/https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.transistor.fm%2F334eea43%2F3bd4b406.mp3&podcastId=6584064), [Immanuel Prayer Training](https://www.anglicandoma.org/messenger-articles/immanuel-prayer), [Immanuel Journaling overview](https://susankuenzi.substack.com/p/immanuel-journaling), [Virkler, 4 Keys](https://ebooks.faithlife.com/product/36940/4-keys-to-hearing-gods-voice), [Kraft, Deep Wounds, Deep Healing](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/65581244), [Charles H. Kraft (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_H._Kraft), [Inner Healing overview](https://www.itsforfreedom.com/inner-healing).

Note: the Winship, Eldredge, Willard and Kraft details come partly from memory and short web summaries. That is acceptable under the inspired-by approach, since we aren't reproducing their teaching. We still don't attribute to them anything we haven't confirmed, and the References page points to the books.

### On Kraft specifically

Kraft's method is memory-based. The summaries agree on the core: memories are stored as pictures, so they are healed with pictures; the person asks the Holy Spirit to bring the memory He wants to heal, pictures Jesus there, and gives Him the hurt. Kraft also ties inner healing closely to spiritual warfare: forgiveness clears what he calls "garbage," and he teaches evicting demons that attach to it. The app takes the first part and leaves out the second. A form cannot discern demonization, and it should not try. See Safety.

The "continue until healing happens" idea is kept, but as permission to return, not pressure to push through. Healing is God's pace, and the person can stop at any point.

## Information architecture

Practice is the app. Three main places, plus a small More menu.

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Practice** | The start screen and the guided paths. Connected (the breath) is the first step of every session. |
| **What's in the way?** | Two frames behind a toggle, opening on this one: **What's in the way?** (Answering Objections) and **Why practice hearing from God?** (The Case, How He speaks, The With-nesses, The Life, Scripture). |
| **Review** | Past sessions, the person's own "God answered" list, and export to PDF. |
| **More** | **Common questions** (quiet, browse only), References (the books, with Amazon links), Settings (theme, type size, reduced motion, tone, Guidance), and About. |

### Start screen

The first screen is Practice's home. A calm page with one primary button, **Begin**, that opens Connected and then Quick. Below it, quietly, the other ways to pray: Deeper, Healing, Daily, Journaling, each with one line of description, so a seasoned person can go straight to what they want. Returning users see their last session and a **Continue** link if one is unfinished. Small links to *Why practice?* and *What's in the way?* sit at the foot, not in the way.

### Connected

- Reuses the breath component from IAmHere (`breathView` in `IAmHere/app.js`), with the same reduced-motion and tone settings.
- One orienting line from Scripture, rotating among a few (see Scripture content).
- Ends with a single button: "I'm here. Continue." It leads to Practice with the breath already done, so a session can start from it.
- Can also be used on its own with no Practice after.

### Practice

**Begin** runs Connected, then Quick. The other paths are one tap from the start screen. Guidance fades by use (see *One experience, training wheels that fade*), and a **Not sure?** chip on every step opens the matching *What's in the way?* card as a sheet.

**Quick (Winship).** The default for a first-time person.

1. *What are you bringing to Him?* One thing, in the person's own words.
2. Invite: "God, what do You want me to know about this?" Then a quiet, non-clickable line, **Reach, then listen.**, and a box: *What came?*
3. "God, what do You want me to do?" Then a box.
4. *Is there more?* If yes, loop to a free-form "Ask Him anything" step with a box. If no, go to Test.
5. **Test** (see below).
6. **Keep:** one line they'll take with them.

**Deeper (Lehman).**

1. Glad memory: "Is there a memory that is simply good?" Then guided re-entry prompts (what do you see, hear, feel, who is there) with a box.
2. Appreciation: "What do you appreciate most?" Stay until it's felt. A "Not yet" option keeps them here rather than advancing.
3. Welcome: "Jesus, I know You were with me here. I welcome You now. Help me perceive Your presence." Then: *Describe whatever comes, even if slight.*
4. Bring the thing, then the Quick questions.
5. Test, Keep.
6. A persistent **Back to the glad place** button on every step after 1. It returns to step 2.

**A note on every path that asks the person to remember.** *"You never have to picture anything. Remembering, noticing or simply asking is enough. Skip any step that asks for more than you want to give."*

**Healing (Kraft).** For a person who wants to ask God for a memory He wants to heal. It sits behind Connected and the glad place, and says so.

1. **Ready check.** Breath done? Glad place found? If not, send them there first. Remind them they can stop at any time and that this is not for the worst things alone.
2. **Ask.** "Holy Spirit, is there a memory You want to heal today?" Wait. *What came?* A thought, image, feeling, a place or a person all count. If nothing comes, they may ask again, or choose a different path. Nothing is not a failure.
3. **See it.** Just enough, in pictures: where are you, how old, who is there, what is happening. A short box, not a full replay.
4. **Where is Jesus?** "Jesus, where were You?" Then: *What do you see or sense?* Not seeing is allowed. Ask: "What is in the way?"
5. **Give Him the hurt.** "Tell Him what it was like. Give it to Him." *What happened when you did?*
6. **Forgive, if it comes up.** A prompt only: "Is there anyone you need to forgive? Ask Jesus if you are ready, and what He wants." No pressure and no skipping ahead. Forgiving is the person's own choice, not a step to complete.
7. **Lies and truth.** "What did you come to believe about yourself or God in that moment?" Then: "Jesus, what is true?" Record both. The truth is what the person receives, not what the app supplies.
8. **Is it done?** *How does that memory feel now?* A simple scale (heavy / lighter / peace) with a note.
   - Peace: go to Test and Keep.
   - Lighter or heavy: **Ask again.** "Jesus, is there more here, or something else You want to heal?" This loops back to step 4, 5 or 7, or to step 2 for another memory, as the person chooses.
9. **Come back.** If it is heavy or the person is tired, the app offers the glad place and a rest, and saves the session as unfinished. Reopening an unfinished healing session starts at the ready check, then picks up where they stopped. There is no limit on how many times they return, and no score for finishing.

Every step after 2 has the **Back to the glad place** button. A **Stop and rest** button is always visible.

**Daily.** A short, repeatable path for ordinary moments: morning, before a decision (the prompt asks for wisdom and the next faithful step, not a yes or no: *"What do You want me to know about this? What is the next faithful step?"*), after Scripture, praying for someone, evening. See *Daily path* below.

**Journaling (Wilder).** Written as the person receiving, step by step, with the Exodus-modeled template. Needs its own careful wording and a clear note that the person is writing what they perceive, not dictating what God must say.

#### Inputs

- Each box accepts typing. A mic button appears where `SpeechRecognition` or `webkitSpeechRecognition` exists.
- Dictation on iOS PWAs is unreliable, and the keyboard's own dictation key works anyway. The app treats the mic as optional, shows it only when supported, and never depends on it.
- Following the repo's rule on mobile keyboards: do not lay out the UI around the iOS keyboard. Each step should read well before the keyboard opens, with the box near the top and the buttons not pinned to the bottom.
- Drafts autosave so a lock-screen interruption loses nothing.

#### How did it come? (optional)

After the person writes an answer, a quiet optional row asks **How did it come?** with tap-to-select chips: *A thought · A verse · A picture or memory · A feeling or peace · A person · Something that happened · Not sure · Nothing yet.* Each chip links to its entry in **How He speaks**.

- Always skippable. "Not sure" is a normal answer and never an error.
- Shown with guidance on, and collapsed to a small link once guidance fades.
- Stored with the answer. In Review a private, on-device note shows patterns over time: "You've noticed God mostly through quiet thoughts and verses." It never scores or ranks, and never says what the pattern means.
- It does two jobs: it teaches newcomers what counts, and it gives seasoned people a way to see how they actually hear.

#### Test step

The second hand of the reach. The line at the top reads: *You reached. Now test.* Offered as **"Three things to look at, when you're ready,"** plus a fourth, each a tap (yes / not sure / no) with a note field. A lamp, not a grade.

1. **Scripture.** Does it agree with what Scripture says?
2. **Jesus.** Does it point to Jesus? Does it make me proud, or demand that I be sure?
3. **Love.** Does it lead toward love for God and for people? Peace can take time, and conviction can be real. If I can't tell, I don't decide alone.
4. **Someone I trust.** Have I told someone I trust?

Rules for what the app says in response:
- **Content decides, not feeling.** Heaviness or peace alone never settles who something is from. The app never tells a person that a feeling proves it is or isn't God.
- **If any answer is "no" on the first two:** the app says plainly that this is a reason to set it down and talk with a pastor, priest, spiritual director or mature friend, and offers the glad place or a break.
- **If "not sure," or the person can't tell on the third:** "Hold it lightly. You don't have to decide alone. Bring it to someone you trust."
- **If the note field or answers suggest the person is distressed or in danger:** follow the safety rules, not this step.
- **If the person has answered "not sure" repeatedly across sessions:** a gentle line that this can be a heavy way to read oneself, that rest is allowed, and that a counselor or pastor can help. This guards against the scrupulous spiral the review warned about.

#### Keep

One line, in the person's words: "What will you keep?" Optionally a small next step and an optional reminder (see below).

Two quiet notes sit under the Keep box. They fade with guidance:
- *"This is your attempt to listen. It isn't God's dictation. Hold it loosely."*
- *"If it touches your marriage, your children, your health or medication, a large sum of money, or a choice that can't be undone, don't act on it alone and don't act on it quickly. Ask the people who love you."*

### What's in the way? Answering Objections

This is the content of the *What's in the way?* frame. The frame is the flow (the toggle side and the **Not sure?** sheet). **Answering Objections** is the section of cards inside it.

**The one question.** *Does He really want to talk with me?* Only the barriers that keep a person from saying yes belong here. Everything else is handled where it is needed, not advertised as an objection. A long list of objections teaches people there are many reasons to doubt, which is the opposite of the goal, so this list stays short on purpose.

**Six cards.** Gentle first.

**Where everything else lives** (full drafts are kept in `parked/objection-cards-full-draft.md`):
- **Nowhere in the app: the Care page.** A "When it's heavy" page (frightening experiences, mental illness and medication, grief, spiritual hurt, anger) was drafted and then removed at the author's direction: leading with care and safety moved the app toward managed self-care and away from faith. The Safety page is sufficient. The last draft is parked in `parked/care-page-draft.md`.
- **Practice notes at the right step**: big life decisions (Keep step), asking about decisions (Daily prompt), "this is your attempt to listen, not God's dictation" (Keep note), and no picturing required (the remember steps).
- **More > Common questions** (quiet, browse only): imagination and New Age, Jesus "in the room," arrogance, prophecy and Hebrews.
- **Why practice? > The Case and The Reach of Faith**: the arguments for those who want them.

Wording is **draft** and needs real pastors. No card claims more than Scripture does.

**Card rules (from the pastoral review):**
- **Companion voice, not debate voice.** Argument lives in *Why practice?*. A card may point there, never argue at length.
- **Content tests lead; feelings never decide alone.** Does it agree with Scripture, does it honor Jesus, does it ask me to hurt anyone or hide something. Heaviness or peace alone does not tell the person who a thing is from. When in doubt, **don't decide alone.**
- **No "it fades" test.** Persistence is not evidence of God.
- **"May" and "often,"** never "usually" or "always."
- **Name the people to test with in each tradition's words:** pastor, priest, spiritual director, spiritual father, elder, or a mature friend.
- **No 988 in a card.** It appears only after a worrisome input.
- **Each card:** the question in the person's words, a short answer, verses that carry the point in context, and a small **Try this** where it fits.
- **The Not sure? sheet offers only these six**, plus the Nothing came? step. Nothing else ever interrupts a prayer.

**Disclaimers are not repeated here.** The one soft line about the app being a prayer aid lives in About and Safety (see Safety). It is not shown at the foot of this area or on first launch.

#### The six

**"I'm not sure God is there, or that He'd talk with me."** *(sheet)*
Yes. You don't need to be certain. A man once told Jesus, "I believe; help my unbelief," and Jesus helped him. You can treat this as asking honestly and seeing what happens. No one is asking you to pretend. You can also just sit quietly and say nothing at all. That's a prayer too.
*Mark 9:24, Matthew 7:7.* **Try this:** "God, if You're there, I'd like to know You." [draft]

**"I'm not good enough for Him to speak to me."** *(sheet)*
God's love isn't a reward for being good. The father in Jesus' story ran to meet a son who had wasted everything, before he had finished his speech. You are invited to come near. If you are carrying something specific, you can tell Him. He already knows.
*Luke 15:20, Romans 5:8, Hebrews 4:16.* [draft]

**"Isn't this just for pastors or mystics?"** *(sheet)*
Jesus talked about His sheep, not His staff. The new covenant promise is that all of them will know Him, from the least to the greatest. Hearing Him grows with practice, like any friendship, and quiet seasons are part of that too.
*John 10:3–5, Romans 8:14, Jeremiah 31:34 (knowing Him).* [draft]

**"I tried and nothing happened."** *(sheet)*
That's common, and it doesn't mean something is wrong with you. Many faithful people have had long quiet seasons. Elijah, after his worst week, was first fed and allowed to sleep, and only then heard a whisper. Sometimes the kindest thing is rest. You can try the "Nothing came?" page, or just thank Him for something and come back another day. If the silence hurts, tell Him that. The Psalms do.
*1 Kings 19:5–13, Psalm 13:1–2, Habakkuk 2:1.* **Try this:** "Nothing came?" [draft]

**"Hasn't God already spoken in the Bible? My church doesn't teach this."** *(sheet)*
The Bible is God's word and the standard for everything. Nothing here adds to it, and if something you hear disagrees with it, put it down. Jesus also said His sheep hear His voice, and He promised the Spirit would teach and remind. Christians have understood this in different ways, and many good churches teach it differently. You don't have to leave yours or take on a label. If you try this, bring what you write to your pastor, priest, spiritual director, or someone mature in your church, and let them help you weigh it.
*Acts 17:11, John 10:27, John 14:26, Proverbs 11:14.* **See:** The Case. [draft]

**"How do I know it's Him, and not me or something else?"** *(sheet)*
It may be your own thoughts. Often that's how God speaks, through a mind He made. You don't have to be sure. Ask: does it agree with Scripture? Does it honor Jesus? Does it ask me to hurt anyone, or to hide something from people who love me? Write it down, and tell someone you trust. If it asks something big, wait. If it only leaves you feeling heavy, that alone doesn't tell you who it's from. When you can't tell, don't decide alone. Put it down and come back another day.
*1 Thessalonians 5:20–21, 1 John 4:1–3.* **See:** The Reach of Faith. **Try this:** the Test step. [draft]

#### The close

*"Is something else in the way? Tell Him. That is a fine first prayer."* A **Try this** opens a blank box: *What's in the way?* The person writes it to God and carries on.

#### Standing check

Offered as **"Three things to look at, when you're ready,"** after a session and at the foot of this area. A lamp, not a grade.

1. **Does it agree with Scripture?**
2. **Does it point to Jesus?** And does it make me proud, or demand that I be sure?
3. **Does it lead toward love for God and for people?** Peace can take time, and conviction can be real. If I can't tell, I don't decide alone.
4. **Have I told someone I trust?**

### The Case (in Why practice?)

A stepped argument, one short screen per step, each ending with the next. Every step is built on texts people can open in the verse popover. Draft wording follows, and every reference must be read in context before shipping.

**Step 1. From the beginning, God talks with the people He loves.**
He walked in the garden and spoke with Adam and Eve (Genesis 3:8–9). He spoke with Abraham, called "the friend of God" (Isaiah 41:8, James 2:23). He spoke with Moses "face to face, as a man speaks to his friend" (Exodus 33:11). Samuel was a boy when the LORD called him by name (1 Samuel 3). Speaking is how this God relates to His people. The only question is whether that stopped.

**Step 2. The promise of the new covenant is that this goes wider, not narrower.**
Moses wished that all the LORD's people were prophets and that He would put His Spirit on them (Numbers 11:29). Jeremiah promised a covenant in which "they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest" (Jeremiah 31:33–34, quoted in Hebrews 8:10–11). Joel promised the Spirit on all flesh, and Peter said at Pentecost that this was now (Joel 2:28–29, Acts 2:16–18). It is a promise about everyday believers, not about a gifted few.

**Step 3. Jesus said it plainly, and He said it to ordinary people.**
- "My sheep hear My voice, and I know them, and they follow Me" (John 10:27; also 10:3–5, 10:16).
- "I have called you friends, for all that I have heard from my Father I have made known to you" (John 15:15). Friends are told what the Master is doing, and servants aren't.
- "No one knows the Father except the Son and anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal Him" (Matthew 11:27).
- The Spirit "will teach you all things and bring to your remembrance all that I have said" (John 14:26), "will guide you into all the truth" and "declare to you the things that are to come" (John 16:13).
Jesus did not teach this as a secret. He taught it as ordinary life with Him.

**Step 4. Jesus lived this way Himself.**
"The Son can do nothing of His own accord, but only what He sees the Father doing" (John 5:19). "I do nothing on My own authority, but speak just as the Father taught Me" (John 8:28). He rose early to pray (Mark 1:35) and spent whole nights in prayer before choosing the Twelve (Luke 6:12). If the Son of God lived by listening, listening isn't beneath us.

**Step 5. The apostles and the early church expected and practiced it.**
It happens on almost every page of Acts: Philip is told to go to the chariot (8:26, 29). Ananias, an otherwise unknown disciple, hears the Lord in a vision, answers, and even argues (9:10–16). Peter is told, "Behold, three men are looking for you" (10:19). The Spirit speaks while the church worships (13:2), forbids a route and gives a vision (16:6–10), and shows a prophet what's coming (21:10–11). The council writes, "It seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us" (15:28). These were not the apostles only. Ananias and the believers at Antioch were ordinary.

**Step 6. The letters assume every believer can hear, and tell us to practice.**
- "All who are led by the Spirit of God are sons of God" (Romans 8:14). You can't be led without hearing.
- "The Spirit Himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God" (Romans 8:16). "God has sent the Spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying 'Abba! Father!'" (Galatians 4:6).
- "We have received the Spirit who is from God, that we might understand the things freely given us by God… we have the mind of Christ" (1 Corinthians 2:12, 16).
- "You have been anointed by the Holy One… His anointing teaches you about everything" (1 John 2:20, 27).
- "Earnestly desire the spiritual gifts, especially that you may prophesy" (1 Corinthians 14:1). "You can all prophesy one by one" (14:31).
- "Today, if you hear His voice, do not harden your hearts" (Hebrews 3:7–8, quoting Psalm 95). That is written to believers, as a standing instruction.
- "Keep in step with the Spirit" (Galatians 5:25). "Do not quench the Spirit" (1 Thessalonians 5:19).
- "He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches" (Revelation 2:7, and six more times in chapters 2–3).
- Discernment is learned by use: "those who have their powers of discernment trained by constant practice" (Hebrews 5:14). Scripture expects a practiced ear, and that means practice.
Hearing is assumed, commanded and trained. That does not mean a person who hears little is failing. Quiet seasons are part of the walk, and Scripture has them too (see *What's in the way?*).

**Step 7. Scripture also tells us to test everything, which is why we do.**
"Test everything; hold fast what is good" (1 Thessalonians 5:21). "Do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits" (1 John 4:1). Let the others "weigh what is said" (1 Corinthians 14:29). The Bereans were praised for checking even Paul against the Scriptures (Acts 17:11). Testing is not a reason to avoid hearing. It is how hearing is kept safe. That is the Test step in every path.

**Step 8. The strongest objections, answered.**

*"Hebrews 1:1–2 says God 'has spoken to us by His Son.' Isn't that the end?"*
Yes. The Son is God's final and complete word, and nothing here adds to Him. But the same Son says the Spirit will take what is His and declare it to us (John 16:13–15). The writer of Hebrews goes on to say, "Today, if you hear His voice" (3:7). Final revelation and ongoing hearing are not opposed.

*"Revelation 22:18–19 says not to add to the book."*
Right. Hearing God does not add to Scripture, and it never gets the same authority. It doesn't give new doctrine. It applies what is already given to a particular life, as every sermon and prayer does. Closing the canon is not the same as God going quiet.

*"2 Timothy 3:16–17 says Scripture equips for every good work."*
It does, and it never says that God has stopped speaking in any other way. Scripture itself shows God speaking beyond what was then written, and the Bereans tested every word, including Paul's, against Scripture.

*"1 Corinthians 13:8–10 says prophecy will cease."*
It does: "when the perfect comes." Paul explains what that means in verse 12: "then face to face." That has not happened. We still see in a mirror dimly, so we still need what helps us now. Faithful Christians read this differently, and this is the reading we hold.

*"Isn't it dangerous?"*
It can be, and that is why Scripture adds the test. Counterfeits are not an argument against the real. They are an argument for discernment. The answer to forged banknotes is not to stop using money.

**Step 9. A word about what is and isn't claimed.**
Scripture teaches that God speaks and that we are to listen, test and obey. It does not prescribe the particular forms this app offers: a breath, a glad memory, a set of questions, a journal. Those are practical help drawn from teachers who have found them useful. Use what helps. Leave what doesn't.

**Last screen.** "That's the case. If it holds, the next question is how. Want to try one question?" → Connected, then Quick.

Notes:
- The Case never calls opponents unfaithful. It says: faithful Christians disagree, and here is why we believe this reading is right.
- Verses are quoted briefly. Full text through the popover.
- The numbered steps are screens, not a wall. A progress line reads "Step 3 of 9."

### The Reach of Faith (in Why practice?)

The idea this page carries: **every kind of hearing God is a reach of faith.** That includes Scripture, which nobody debates. Hearing is never just receiving data. It is trusting that Someone is speaking, perceiving what He said, and responding to something stirred within us. This takes the sting out of "isn't inner hearing just subjective?", because the same reach is already part of every way of hearing. It also tells a newcomer what is being asked of them: not certainty first, but a reach.

It sits right after The Case: first that God speaks, then what hearing Him always involves.

**What it claims, and what it does not.**
- It claims that the *posture* is the same for Scripture and for a quiet impression: trust in the listening, in the message, and in the sender, and a response.
- It does not claim the *authority* is the same. Scripture is the measure. Everything else is measured by it. See The Case, step 7.
- It does not make Scripture subjective. Scripture's meaning is what the text says. The page is about what it takes to *receive* it as God's word and be changed by it, a point the church has long called **illumination**.
- It does not belittle study. Careful reading is good, and the Bereans modeled it (Acts 17:11). The point is that study alone isn't yet hearing.

**Screen by screen (draft).**

**1. You've already done this.**
Anyone who has prayed, believed a promise, taken a verse to heart, or felt a prompting to call someone has reached. Hearing God has always been that.

**2. Every hearing is a reach, even between people.**
A letter from a friend, a voice on the phone, a text. You trust the sender, you interpret the words, and you respond. You can't make it airtight first. If you refused to trust until you had proof, you'd never hear anyone. Hearing always involves interpretation, and interpretation always involves trust.

**3. Even Scripture.**
We don't just read the Bible the way we read a manual and call that hearing God. The Bible shows study without hearing, and hearing that needed something more:
- The leaders who searched the Scriptures and still would not come to Jesus (John 5:39–40).
- The two on the Emmaus road, who knew the Scriptures and still didn't see until He opened them to them. Afterward: "Did not our hearts burn within us?" (Luke 24:25–27, 32, 45).
- The Ethiopian reading Isaiah: "How can I, unless someone guides me?" (Acts 8:30–31).
- Lydia, whose heart "the Lord opened to pay attention" (Acts 16:14).
- "The word they heard did not benefit them, because they were not united by faith with those who listened" (Hebrews 4:2).
- The soils: the same seed, different hearing (Matthew 13:1–23). "Take care then how you hear" (Luke 8:18).
- "The natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit of God" (1 Corinthians 2:14). We have "received the Spirit who is from God, that we might understand" (2:12).
- "Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of your law" (Psalm 119:18).
Reading Scripture as God's voice is itself a reach: trusting that He is speaking in it, perceiving what He is saying to me, and answering.

**4. Three trusts in every hearing.**
- **The listening.** That I can actually perceive. "My sheep hear My voice" (John 10:27).
- **The message.** That what I perceived is what was said, which is why we test.
- **The sender.** That it is really Him, and that He is good, and wants to be heard. "Whoever would draw near to God must believe that He exists and that He rewards those who seek Him" (Hebrews 11:6). Also John 7:17.
These can't be proven before we start. They are tested by walking in them.

**5. What stirs, and the answer to it.**
Scripture often describes hearing as something stirred within us, which we then answer.
- "Everyone whose spirit God had stirred" (Ezra 1:5). "The LORD stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel" (Haggai 1:14).
- "You have said, 'Seek My face.' My heart says to You, 'Your face, LORD, do I seek'" (Psalm 27:8). A word, and a heart that answers it.
- People "cut to the heart" (Acts 2:37), and hearts that burned (Luke 24:32).
The practice, then, is to notice what stirs, and to answer it: ask, write, take a small step. Hearing without response isn't completed hearing (James 1:22–25; John 10:27 goes on, "and they follow Me").

**6. A reach is not a leap in the dark.**
Reaching and testing are two hands.
- Reach without testing is credulity.
- Testing without reaching never hears. The leaders had the text and missed the Man.
The Test step in every path is the second hand. Scripture is the measure, mature believers help, and fruit shows over time. The app asks for both.

**7. What we're asking you to do.**
Not to be certain first. To reach: to call Him "Thou," to say "Speak, Lord, I'm listening," and to see what stirs. Then to test, and to answer.

**With-nesses for this page.**
- **Spurgeon** on John 10:27, verified: "They can hear, because they have had spiritual ears given them. Once the Shepherd might have spoken all day long and they would not have heard Him." (Sermon 2120, 1889.)
- **Lewis**, verified: "He speaks as 'I' when we truly call Him 'Thou.'" (*Letters to Malcolm*, Letter 4.) The reach of addressing Him as a person.
- **Tozer**, verified in part: approach the Bible "with the idea that it is not only a book which was once spoken, but a book which is now speaking."
- **Augustine**, verified: "Thou calledst, and shoutedst, and burstest my deafness." (*Confessions* 10.27.) The ear itself had to be opened.
- **Murray**, verified: "Wait on God until you know you have met Him; prayer will then become so different." (*Waiting on God*, Fourth Day.)
- **Chambers**, verified on utmost.org (Modern Classic edition): "Whether or not I hear God's call depends upon the state of my ears; and what I hear depends upon my disposition." ("[Called of God](https://utmost.org/classic/called-of-god-classic/)".) Also, from "[The Devotion of Hearing](https://utmost.org/the-devotion-of-hearing)": "The goal of my spiritual life is such close identification with Jesus Christ that I will always hear God."

**Where this page connects.**
- **In practice.** The pause before each ask carries the plain line **Reach, then listen.** It is not a link. Nothing on a practice screen should send the person away mid-prayer. The page is found from *Why practice?* and from the cards that point to it. No new step.
- **In What's in the way?** The cards "What if I'm making it up?", "Isn't the Bible enough?" and "Isn't hearing just subjective?" link here.
- **In Test.** The step is framed as the second hand: "You reached. Now test."
- **In How He speaks.** The intro line reminds the person that each way is a reach and a test.

**Review notes.** The claim that reading Scripture requires the Spirit's illumination is mainstream (1 Corinthians 2:12–14, Ephesians 1:17–18) and should read as a familiar doctrine applied further, not as a novelty. Pastors reviewing this page should check that it nowhere reads as saying Scripture is subjective or that impressions equal Scripture.

### How He speaks (in Why practice?)

A newcomer's real question is often "What am I even listening for?" This door answers it. It is a map of the ways God speaks that Scripture shows and Christians have reported, ordered from the most everyday to the rarer. For each way: what it is, where Scripture shows it, a witness who knew it, how to test it, and a small thing to try.

Framing, stated once at the top: *"This isn't a rulebook or a ranking. Scripture doesn't give a list, and God isn't limited to one way. These are the common ways He speaks, so you know what to notice. He often uses several together, and every one is tested the same way, and every one is a reach of faith."*

**1. Through Scripture** *(the plainest and most reliable)*
A verse or passage that comes alive, answers the question you brought, or keeps returning to mind.
- *In Scripture:* Luke 24:27, 32 (the Emmaus road: "Did not our hearts burn within us while He talked to us on the road, while He opened to us the Scriptures?"). Hebrews 4:12. Matthew 4:4 (Jesus answered by the Word). Psalm 119:105.
- *With-nesses:* Tozer, "a book which is now speaking." Ambrose, "We hear Him when we read the divine saying." Müller, who sought God's will "through the instrumentality of the Word of God."
- *Test:* It must mean what the passage actually means in context.
- *Try this:* Read a short passage slowly and ask, "What are You saying to me in this?"

**2. A quiet thought or impression** *(the most common)*
A thought that arrives with weight. A word, a phrase, a settled "knowing." It often sounds like your own mind, because the Spirit works through it.
- *In Scripture:* 1 Kings 19:12 (the gentle whisper). Isaiah 30:21 (a word behind you). John 14:26 (He will bring to your remembrance). Acts 8:29, 10:19, 13:2 ("the Spirit said").
- *With-nesses:* Wesley, "an inward impression on the soul." Willard (describes the voice as an impression on the mind). Brother Lawrence, "continually conversing with Him." Chambers, "Speak, Lord."
- *Test:* The Test step. Does it agree with Scripture, point to Jesus, and lead toward love? Write it down, and tell someone you trust.
- *Try this:* Ask one question, wait one breath, and write the first thing that comes.

**3. Peace, and its absence** *(a settled sense)*
A deep rightness or calm about a path, or a quiet unease that won't settle.
- *In Scripture:* Colossians 3:15 (let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts). Philippians 4:7. Romans 8:16 (the Spirit bears witness with our spirit). Acts 16:6–7 (the Spirit of Jesus did not allow). 2 Corinthians 2:12–13 (no rest in his spirit).
- *With-nesses:* Wesley on the witness of the Spirit. Edwards' tests of what the Spirit's work produces (love, humility, a turning toward Jesus).
- *Test:* Anxiety and the Spirit's unrest are not the same. Peace from the Spirit is deeper than mood and does not demand certainty. Be honest about fatigue, hunger and fear.
- *Try this:* Hold a decision before Him and notice what your body and spirit do.

**4. Another person** *(a friend, a pastor, a stranger with the right word)*
Someone says what you needed, or confirms what you've been sensing.
- *In Scripture:* 1 Samuel 3:8–9 (Eli helps Samuel recognize the voice). Acts 9:17 (Ananias to Saul). Acts 21:10–11 (Agabus). 1 Corinthians 14:3, 29–31. Proverbs 11:14.
- *With-nesses:* Samuel's story. Müller's warning that counsel of others is never to outrank the Word of God.
- *Test:* Weigh it as Scripture asks. People can be wrong, and the confirmation of mature believers helps.
- *Try this:* Share what you wrote with one trusted person and ask what they notice.

**5. Circumstances: open and closed doors**
Events line up, or something blocks the way, and both turn out to matter.
- *In Scripture:* 1 Corinthians 16:8–9 (a wide door and many adversaries). Colossians 4:3. Revelation 3:8. Acts 16:6–10 (blocked, then a vision, then a door).
- *With-nesses:* To source. Candidates include Müller's own accounts of prayer and provision.
- *Test:* Circumstances alone interpret poorly. Use them to confirm what you've heard some other way, not to replace it.
- *Try this:* Look back over the last week. Where did something open, close, or arrive just when you needed it?

**6. Pictures, memories and dreams**
An image, a remembered scene, or a dream that carries meaning. This is the way the app's *remember* prompts lean on, and the one many people find hardest.
- *In Scripture:* Acts 10:9–16 (Peter's vision). Acts 16:9–10 (Paul's vision). Matthew 1:20, 2:12 (dreams). Joel 2:28, Acts 2:17.
- *With-nesses:* Ignatius' practice of praying a Gospel scene with the imagination. Virkler lists it among his keys.
- *Test:* Never required. The app never asks anyone to invent an image. If something comes, write it. If not, that's fine. Test it like everything else, and hold it loosely.
- *Try this:* Remember one place where you felt God was near, and notice what stands out.

**7. Conviction and comfort**
Conviction is a specific, clear, loving "this is not you" about something. It leads to repentance and relief, not to shame. Comfort is the quiet word that you are held.
- *In Scripture:* John 16:8 (He convicts). Acts 2:37. 2 Corinthians 7:10 (godly grief). Romans 8:1 (no condemnation). John 14:16–18 (the Comforter, not left as orphans).
- *With-nesses:* Fox, "There is one, even Christ Jesus, that can speak to thy condition." Spurgeon, the sheep who "distinguish between His voice and other voices."
- *Test:* This is where the voices differ most. The Spirit convicts specifically and leads to Jesus. Accusation is general, crushing and sends you away from Him. If it condemns, it isn't Him.
- *Try this:* Ask, "Is there anything You want me to see?" and notice whether what comes draws you toward Him or away.

**8. Rarer ways** *(not to be sought, not to be feared)*
A spoken word, a prophetic word through another believer, a word of knowledge, a clear visitation.
- *In Scripture:* 1 Samuel 3:4–10. Acts 9:4–6. 1 Corinthians 12:7–11, 14:24–25.
- *With-nesses:* Justin Martyr and Irenaeus, who said the gifts continued in the church of their day. Fox, whose account is of hearing a voice.
- *Test:* Held to the strictest test. The more unusual the claim, the more it needs mature confirmation. None of this is required for a real walk with God.
- *Try this:* Nothing. If this happens, bring it to someone mature.

**How the ways work together.** God doesn't use one channel alone. A verse comes, a friend says the same thing, and peace follows. When different ways agree, trust grows. When one stands alone and unusual, slow down.

**What this door says to the person who has had none of these.** "Hearing is a relationship, not a performance. Start where it's easiest for you. Many people hear first through Scripture or a quiet thought. The rest comes with time."

Notes:
- The list is a teaching aid. Scripture does not catalog ways, and the app says so.
- Each way carries a small icon-free label, and the Scripture list and The With-nesses cards carry the same labels, so they cross-link.
- In practice, an optional **How did it come?** row appears after an answer (see Practice), so the person can learn their own patterns.

### The Life (in Why practice?)

What the interactive life looks like. This part is for the person who has heard the case and wants to see the thing itself. It has four parts, each short.

**1. Ordinary people in Scripture who heard.**
Each is a small card with the verses, two or three lines, and "What this shows."
- **Ananias** (Acts 9:10–19). An ordinary disciple. The Lord speaks to him in a vision, he answers "Here I am," he argues ("Lord, I have heard from many about this man"), the Lord answers him, and he obeys. Shows: a conversation, honest objection allowed, a small obedient step.
- **Samuel** (1 Samuel 3). A boy who didn't recognize the voice, and an older believer who helped him. Shows: it can take help to recognize. Learning is normal. His reply, "Speak, for your servant hears" (1 Samuel 3:10), is the prayer Chambers urges us to make a habit, and the app can offer it as a plain opening line before the first ask.
- **Philip** (Acts 8:26–40). Told to go, then told to join the chariot. Shows: a series of small nudges, each followed.
- **Peter at Joppa** (Acts 10). A vision, then "the Spirit said to him." Shows: God tells him something that changes what he thinks is allowed, and then the Spirit confirms it with events.
- **The church in Antioch** (Acts 13:1–3). A group worshiping and fasting, and the Spirit speaks. Shows: hearing together.
- **Mary** (Luke 10:38–42). She sits and listens. Shows: presence before productivity.
- **Moses** (Exodus 33:11). Spoke with God as a friend. Shows: the aim is friendship.

**2. A day with Him.**
Short, concrete moments. None is required and none is a rule.
- **Morning.** "Good morning. What do You want me to know today?" Write a line.
- **Before a decision.** Small or large: "What do You think? What do You want me to do?" Then wait, write, check against Scripture.
- **Reading Scripture.** After a passage, ask: "What are You saying to me in this?"
- **Praying for someone.** Ask "What do You want me to say, or do for them?" Notice what comes.
- **Work or chores.** A short "Lord, I'm with You in this" and a quick breath.
- **Conflict.** "What do You see here? What do You want me to know about this person?" (This is where Winship's story comes in.)
- **When you've failed.** "I'm sorry." Then listen for the Father's response, which the Bible says is to run toward you (Luke 15:20).
- **Evening.** "Thank You for today. What was You, and what did I miss?"

**3. How He may speak.** See the **How He speaks** door above. This part of The Life gives one line and a link to it.

**4. What it does over time.**
Stated modestly. We describe what the Bible promises and what Christians report, and not what will happen to any one person.
- Peace in place of striving (Philippians 4:6–7)
- Courage and less fear (1 John 4:18)
- Love that reaches enemies
- A deeper sense of being known and loved (Romans 8:15–16)
- Freedom from lies (John 8:32)
- Wisdom for ordinary choices (James 1:5)

Last screen: "You've seen the case and the witnesses. Now ask one question." → Connected, then Quick.

### Daily path (in Practice)

A fifth path, short and repeatable, for the rhythms above. One tap from Home.
1. Choose a moment: Morning, A decision, After Scripture, Praying for someone, Evening, or Other.
2. One prompt for that moment, one box, a Test glance, and a Keep line if wanted.
3. It saves as a lightweight record. Review shows these in a lighter style so they don't swamp the longer sessions.
4. It never nags. The reminder is a single optional `.ics`.

### Stories and "God answered"

The app will not invent testimonies. Two parts, neither a main tab:

- **Stories.** Short accounts from George, and from others only with their explicit permission. They sit inside **Why practice? > The Life**, with attribution as the giver wishes. Each is brief, plain, and ends by pointing back to Jesus, not to the method.
- **God answered.** The person's own record. From Review, a session can be marked "God answered" with a line about how. These collect into a personal list in Review, stored locally and included in the export. This part works with no outside stories at all.

### Review

- A list of sessions, newest first, each showing date, the thing brought, and the Keep line.
- Opening a session shows the full record: the questions, what the person wrote, and the Test answers.
- Edit the Keep line, delete a session, or delete all.
- A **God answered** list collects marked sessions.
- **Export PDF.** A print-styled HTML page built from the session (or a date range), opened in a new view with the print dialog. This is the same approach other pages in this repo already use, and it avoids a PDF library. The app encourages saving a copy, since local storage can be cleared by the browser.
- Also offer a plain-text/markdown export, which is easier to paste elsewhere.
- Show a gentle note on first save: "These stay on this device only. Export a copy to keep them safe."

## Data model

All in `localStorage` under an `with.` prefix, following the IAmHere `store` helper with try/catch around every access.

```
with.settings   { theme, reduceMotion, largeType, tone, reminderHour, guidance: "fade" | "always" | "hide" }
with.sessions   [ Session ]
with.draft      Session | null     // in-progress, autosaved per step
with.seen       { onboarded, notes } // one-time hints
```

```
Session {
  id, started, ended,           // ISO timestamps, device time
  path: "quick" | "deeper" | "healing" | "journaling" | "daily",
  status: "open" | "complete" | "resting",   // resting = stopped and may return
  answered: { note } | null,                 // marked "God answered" in Review
  memory: {                                  // healing path only
    asked, seen, jesus, gave, forgave, lie, truth,
    feel: "heavy" | "lighter" | "peace" | null,
    rounds: [ { feel, note, at } ]           // each ask-again pass
  } | null,
  connected: boolean,           // did they do the breath first
  bringing: string,
  steps: [ { key, prompt, answer, via: "typed" | "dictated",
            how: ["scripture"|"thought"|"picture"|"peace"|"person"|"circumstance"|"unsure"|"nothing"] } ],
  test: { scripture, jesus, fruit, note },   // each: "yes" | "unsure" | "no" | null
  keep: string,
  followUps: [ { prompt, answer } ]          // the "is there more" loop
}
```

Step prompts are stored with the answers so a record stays readable if wording changes in a later version.

## Safety

**Voice rule: God first, always.** Truth about safety is spoken, but in God's terms: Scripture, the Shepherd's voice, the tests of the spirits, and the wise counsel of people who know Him. It is never spoken in clinical or policy language ("a doctor's care, not a prayer form," "a poor place for trauma work," "a trained person is the right help," "this is not counseling or medical care"). Those phrases put managed self-care above the One the app is about and were removed. Safety follows faith. It does not lead it and does not replace it.

- If the person writes or taps something that suggests self-harm or crisis, stop the method and show the quiet stop screen with the 988 line (US) and two lines in faith voice: *"Tell someone who loves you and knows Him, today. You don't have to carry this alone."* and *"He came that you might have life (John 10:10). Stay close to Him, and let the people He has given you stay close to you."* This appears after a worrisome input, never as a standing banner (IAmHere's pattern).
- **The Safety page** (in More) says, in order: His sheep hear His voice and won't follow a stranger, so test what you hear (John 10:3–5, 1 John 4:1); what is from Him agrees with Scripture, points to Jesus and leads toward love, and never tells you to harm yourself or anyone, hide something from people who love you, or turn from Christ; God is not a God of confusion but of peace, and a voice that accuses and crushes, drives you from Jesus, commands harm or won't let go is not the Shepherd's, so don't obey it, put it down, bring it to Jesus, and ask Him to speak what is true (1 Corinthians 14:33); don't carry it alone, bring it to someone mature in the faith (Proverbs 11:14), and where the body needs tending a physician is one of His gifts too (Colossians 4:14); some things are heavy, He is gentle with the bruised reed and not in a hurry (Isaiah 42:3), take one step at a time with someone who knows Him beside you, and you can stop at any time; then the 988 line.
- Deeper and Healing: if it gets heavy, the glad-place button is always visible and the app never asks the person to go further into a painful memory. Healing carries the note: *"Go gently. Take only what He puts in front of you, one step at a time, with someone who knows Him beside you."* Healing is gated behind the breath and the glad place and does not start from the person's worst memories. Rest screen: *"You can stop at any time. He is not in a hurry. Come back when you are ready."*
- No deliverance or demonization content. Kraft's spiritual-warfare teaching is out of scope. If a person describes things beyond a prayer aid, the app points them to a trusted pastor or mature believer.
- "Keep asking until healed" is never a demand. The only measure is the person's own report, and "I'm done for today" is always a valid ending.
- Never ask the app's user to forgive as a requirement, and never imply that holding back forgiveness is failure.
- The wording must not promise that God will speak, or that silence means anything is wrong.
- **No Care page.** There is no "When it's heavy" page and no care links at the top of or inside practice screens. Care and safety do not lead the app. The removed draft is parked in `parked/care-page-draft.md`.
- **More > Common questions** (quiet, browse only, never in the sheet): imagination and New Age; Jesus "in the room"; arrogance; prophecy and Hebrews. Wording from the parked draft (`parked/objection-cards-full-draft.md`), to be reviewed.
- **Disclaimers stay light.** There is no first-launch card. The start screen carries a permanent line under the wordmark, never dismissed: *"With"* with *"God with us (Immanuel, Matthew 1:23)."* The one note about the app's place lives in About and on the Safety page: *"With is a way to practice listening to Him. It doesn't take the place of church, or of the people God has put around you."* No first-run wall of cautions, no "you need a church" instruction, no "not counseling or medical care" language.

## Definition of done

Statuses used in the tracker at the end: **Designed** (in this document), **Drafted** (wording exists), **Built**, **Reviewed**, **Verified**, **Done**.

### Global criteria (apply to everything)

- Works fully offline after the first load. No accounts, no network calls beyond loading the app. Scripture text ships in the app (Berean Standard Bible, public domain).
- All data stays on the device. Nothing is sent anywhere. Local storage reads and writes are wrapped in try/catch, and every screen works if storage is blocked.
- Works in light, dark and auto themes, with large type and reduced motion respected, at phone width with no horizontal scroll.
- Plain-language rules are followed. No banned vocabulary, the app never says what God said, and no promise of results.
- No streaks, scores, badges or nagging anywhere.
- No "draft," "pending" or "awaiting review" wording appears anywhere on screen (a test checks this).
- Passes the in-browser tests page and a manual pass on a **real iPhone** installed as a PWA. Desktop emulation does not count for keyboard, dictation or install behavior.
- Layouts read well before the keyboard opens. Nothing is positioned around the iOS keyboard.
- Service worker updates never swap code under an open session.

### Per aspect

**App shell and PWA**
- Installs from the browser, launches standalone, has icon and manifest, and shows the version from `sw.js` in Settings.
- Update flow waits until the person is on a resting screen.
- Tests page covers the pure logic in `engine.js`.

**Start screen and Practice engine**
- Begin → breath → first question in two taps, with no reading or setup.
- Other paths are listed quietly on the start screen with one line each.
- Step runner autosaves each step, resumes after a lock-screen or app kill, and shows a step counter.
- A mic button appears only where speech recognition exists, and the app works fully without it.
- Every box accepts one word. Sentence starters are available.

**Connected (breath)**
- Breath exercise reused from IAmHere with its reduced-motion and tone settings.
- One orienting line, rotating, plus the Murray caption.
- "I'm here. Continue." leads into Practice. Connected can also be used alone.

**Guidance fade**
- Guidance shows for roughly the first three completed sessions, then collapses. *Fade / Always show / Hide* works in Settings.
- **Not sure?** and **Nothing came?** are always visible, whatever the setting.

**Quick (Winship-inspired)**
- Complete flow: bring, ask what to know, ask what to do, is there more, Test, Keep.
- Finishable in about five minutes, and finishable with "nothing yet" as a valid answer.

**Deeper (Lehman-inspired)**
- Glad memory, appreciation, welcome, bring, Test, Keep.
- "Not yet" holds the person at appreciation. **Back to the glad place** is on every step after the first.

**Healing (Kraft-inspired)**
- Ready check blocks entry until breath and glad place are done.
- **Stop and rest** is always visible. Sessions can be saved as resting and resumed from the ready check.
- No deliverance or demonization content anywhere. Forgiveness is a prompt, never a required step.
- "Ask again" loops work. There is no limit on returns and no completion score.
- **Reviewed by a qualified human before release** (see gates).

**Daily**
- Moment chooser, one prompt, one box, Test glance, optional Keep.
- Saves as a light record that does not swamp longer sessions in Review.

**Journaling (Wilder-inspired)**
- Seven-step form with its own wording, framed as writing what the person perceives, never dictating what God must say.
- **Reviewed by a qualified human before release.**

**Test, Keep and "How did it come?"**
- Test appears in every path, framed as "You reached. Now test." It uses the four questions, and the app never says that a feeling proves something is or isn't God. A "no" on the first two gives the set-it-down guidance, "not sure" gives the hold-it-lightly guidance, and repeated "not sure" across sessions triggers the gentle rest-and-counselor line.
- Keep is one line.
- "How did it come?" is optional, always skippable, never an error, and each chip links to **How He speaks**.
- The Review pattern note never scores, ranks or interprets.

**Not sure? sheet**
- Present on every practice step. Opens the matching card as a bottom sheet and returns to the same step with the draft intact.

**What's in the way? (Answering Objections)**
- Six cards only, each a barrier to believing He wants to talk with us: not sure He's there, not good enough, pastors or mystics, nothing happened, Bible and church, how do I know it's Him. Each has a short answer and verified verses, and the close tells the person to say what's in the way to Him.
- No card claims more than Scripture does. Where Christians disagree, it is said once and fairly.
- The Not sure? sheet offers only those six cards and the Nothing came? step.
- Cards use the people-to-test-with language for each tradition: pastor, priest, spiritual director, spiritual father, elder, mature friend.
- **Reviewed by qualified humans before release** (see gates).

**Why practice hearing from God?**
- *The Case:* each step's verses are read in context and accurate. The strongest objections (Hebrews 1, Revelation 22, 2 Timothy 3, 1 Corinthians 13) have fair answers.
- *The Reach of Faith:* nowhere reads as "Scripture is subjective" or "impressions equal Scripture."
- *How He speaks:* each way has a description, examples, a witness where one is verified, a test and a try-this. Circumstances either gets a verified witness or is shown without one.
- *The With-nesses:* every quote verified from an online page with the translation or edition named, no long quotations from modern works, and context notes where a quote could mislead.
- *The Life:* Scripture portraits checked in context. Stories included only with permission.
- *Scripture:* every verse checked in context, with BSB text in the popover.
- Nothing in this area is a prerequisite. Each door is short and skippable.

**Review and export**
- List, detail, edit Keep, delete one or all, and the God answered list.
- PDF export through a print-styled page and a plain text export both work on a real iPhone.
- First-save note explains that sessions stay on the device and encourages an export.

**References and More**
- Every Amazon link checked and working. Inspired-by note present. No affiliate tag.
- Settings: theme, type size, reduced motion, tone, Guidance, reminder `.ics`.

**Common questions**
- Quiet, browse-only, in More. Never offered by the Not sure? sheet.

**Safety**
- 988 shown only after a worrisome input, never as a standing banner.
- The voice-hearing distinction is explicit.
- The start screen carries the permanent name line, *God with us (Immanuel, Matthew 1:23).*, with "Matthew 1:23" as a tap target that opens the verse popover (the same pattern as IAmHere, showing the ESV text and the Crossway notice). There is no first-launch card and no "Got it" button. The prayer-aid line lives once in About and on the Safety page.
- Heavy-path off-ramps tested by walking each path with a fragile-user scenario.

## Scope and build order

Everything ships as one app. There are no staged releases. The order below is only the order of work, so each piece can be tested before the next depends on it.

1. Shell: routing, storage, settings, theme, service worker, manifest, tests harness.
2. Start screen, Connected with the breath component, the guidance-fade logic, and the "Nothing came?" step.
3. Practice engine: step runner, autosave drafts, resume, optional mic, and the **Not sure?** chip with its bottom sheet.
4. Paths, in this order: Quick (Winship), Deeper (Lehman), Healing (Kraft), Journaling (Wilder). Each path reuses the Test and Keep steps. Eldredge's questions are optional follow-ups inside Quick and Journaling.
5. Review: list, detail, edit, delete, "God answered," PDF and text export.
6. *What's in the way?* (Answering Objections), then *Why practice?*: The Case, **The Reach of Faith**, **How He speaks**, and Scripture (content plus the verse popover and the shared labels).
7. *Why practice?*: The With-nesses and The Life, with stories. "God answered" in Review. The Daily path.
8. References page and optional reminder `.ics`.
9. Offline checks and tests on a real iPhone.

Risk note: the Healing path carries the most weight. It gets the most review before it is declared done: safety behavior tested, and a trusted pastor or counselor reading the prompts. Because the app is inspired by Kraft and not a reproduction of him, review is for safety and Scripture, not fidelity to his text.
