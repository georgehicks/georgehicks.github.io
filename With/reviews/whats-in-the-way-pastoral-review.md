## Dear friend,

I read this the way I would read something you wanted me to hand to a woman in my congregation who lost her husband last spring and has stopped praying. That is the reader I keep seeing. Here is what I found.

### 1. What I most admire

- **The posture is right.** "Invitation, not argument." "No pressure." "Never say what silence means." Most listening-prayer material I have seen breaks those rules in the first paragraph. You wrote them down as rules.
- **You separated the principle from the forms** and said so out loud (Step 9, the Position). That one move protects a lot of people.
- **Scripture stays the measure**, and you say it on nearly every card. A Reformed colleague will relax when they see that.
- **The wounded are named.** "Nobody gets to tell you what God said" is the sentence I would want a spiritual-abuse survivor to meet first.
- **"Dear God," and write one line.** That is exactly what I'd tell a nervous person. It is also the Psalms' own way in.
- **Elijah and the whisper, and Samuel not yet knowing the Lord (1 Sam 3:7).** Both are honest uses of the text.
- **Silence is allowed.** Good. Say it more often and more bluntly (see below).

### 2. The three things that worry me most, in order

**First: the tests turn a feeling into the verdict, and the feeling is the thing a fragile person can't trust.**
The cards say a word from God brings peace and not "fear, shame and pressure," that "if it condemns you … it isn't from Him," that "a good word tends to hold up, and one that was only you tends to fade," and that where you are left "condemned, test it again." I understand the intent. But think about who is reading.

- A person with scrupulosity or OCD will feel condemned by almost anything. They will spend the next hour deciding whether their dread is the Spirit's conviction or the enemy's accusation. This is a real, common, painful trap, and these cards hand it to them.
- A depressed person hears condemnation in their own voice all day. The card tells them to test whether it is God. They may conclude that God is not speaking to them *at all*, because everything they hear is heavy.
- Conviction is real. John 16:8 says the Spirit convicts. 2 Corinthians 7:10 says godly grief leads to repentance. Peter was cut to the heart in Acts 2:37. The cards say shame is never from God, and that is true. But they never say that *sorrow over real sin* can be from Him, and a person who is truly in the wrong will take the card as permission to dismiss it. That is the opposite error and it is just as dangerous.
- "A good word tends to hold up, and one that was only you tends to fade" is simply not true to experience. Obsessions persist. Resentments persist. A strong wish persists for years and feels like a calling. Delusions persist. Persistence is not evidence of God. Please cut that sentence. A pastor who wrote it would be embarrassed in a year.
- Romans 8:1 ("no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus") is about the *verdict at the judgment seat*, not about how a person feels. Used as a feeling test, it can be turned on the believer: "I feel condemned, so I must not be in Christ." It needs different handling.

The fix is not to remove the tests. It is to lead with the *content* tests (does it agree with Scripture, does it honor Jesus, does it ask me to hurt anyone or hide something), to say plainly that feelings alone cannot decide, and to tell the person that when in doubt they should *not* decide alone. See my rewording in the card reviews.

**Second: the app has two different answers to "what if I hear something strange?" and neither is safe enough for the people most at risk.**
"What if I hear something frightening or wrong?" says voices "that aren't like your own thoughts" deserve a doctor. But another card says that God's word "may well sound like your own thoughts." So the test for "is this a symptom?" is *how it sounds*, and the same app has just told the reader it often sounds like their own mind. That is confusing for anyone, and dangerous for someone in early psychosis, in mania (religious content is common), or in a severe depression with intrusive thoughts. A pastor does not tell them to judge by the sound. A pastor says: if it frightens you, commands you, or comes with a sense of being watched or chosen, or if you aren't sleeping, call someone today.

Also: the card places the 988 line inside the card text. Your own Safety section (and IAmHere) says that line appears only after a worrisome input, not as a standing line. I'd keep that discipline. A standing 988 line in an objection card tells a sad, curious reader "you might be suicidal" before they have said anything. That is exactly what you have already decided *not* to do elsewhere. The card and the Safety section disagree; the Safety section is right.

This is the only card I'm marking HOLD. It should be rewritten with someone clinical in the room: a Christian counselor, or a psychiatric nurse who attends church. I would not want to carry it alone.

**Third: the argument voice leaks into cards that were meant to be companionship.**
You say "no pressure" and "invitation, not argument," and then several cards (Bible enough, Hebrews/prophecy, subjective, "my church doesn't teach this") argue, in your own voice, a contested position: "We think Scripture itself says otherwise," "this is the reading we hold." You say it honestly, which I respect. But a reader who came with "My church doesn't teach this" is often in a spiritual crisis with their family or elders, and the card ends with a gentle push ("It asks whether Jesus meant what He said in John 10"). That subtly frames their church as the people who aren't taking Jesus at His word. I would not let that sentence stand. More below.

Close runners-up:
- **Verses that don't carry their weight.** Hebrews 8:11 and Jeremiah 31:34 (about *knowing the Lord* universally, not about individual guidance, as I confirmed in context), Romans 8:16 (assurance of adoption, not a test for self-generated thoughts), Matthew 23:4 (hypocritical teachers piling on rules, not people claiming to hear God), Hebrews 4:16 (approaching the throne of grace, not "Jesus in the room"), Colossians 2:8–10 (warning against false philosophy; fits New Age only loosely). Nobody will be harmed, but a sharp Reformed reader will notice, and it spends your credibility, which is the thing you most need.
- **Overclaims where you said you wouldn't.** "Your own thoughts are still where God usually meets you." "Usually" is a claim about God's habits. Language rules say "may" and "often." Hold to it.

### 3. Objections I've heard from real people that are missing

I'd add these, roughly in order of how often they come up:

1. **"I'm grieving. Can I hear from my husband / my child?"** The honest answer is no, and gently. Scripture forbids seeking the dead (Deut 18:10–12) and the app should say it is for hearing *God*, and that grief itself is welcome in prayer. This is the one I fear most, because grief and listening prayer are close neighbors.
2. **"I have anxiety, OCD, depression, bipolar or trauma. Is this safe for me?"** Currently only voices are addressed. Many more people will read this card than hear voices. The honest answer is "yes, gently, and tell your counselor or doctor, and don't stop your medication because of something you hear." Medication needs its own sentence. Some pastors have done real damage here.
3. **"What if I think God is telling me to leave my spouse / quit my job / confront someone / give money?"** The card "What do I do with what I hear?" half-answers it ("costly or irreversible, wait"). Name the big ones: marriage, children, medication, money, confronting someone, a call to ministry. These are exactly the places where a "word" gets people hurt.
4. **"God told my friend / pastor / spouse something about me."** This is the real injury behind half the abuse stories. The "hurt" card helps but doesn't name the case: *someone else's word about you is a thing to weigh, not obey.*
5. **"Is it arrogant to say God speaks to me?"** Many humble people believe it is. It's the quietest form of "I'm not good enough" and is not quite covered.
6. **"Why would He speak to me but not heal my child / stop what happened to me?"** Not strictly an objection to the practice, but it is in the room for many, and silence can feel like the same abandonment. The card on silence needs to know it exists.
7. **"Can I ask Him about decisions? About the lottery, an exam, whether someone loves me?"** Practical, frequent, and where prayer slides toward divination. A pastor says: ask for wisdom and for His presence; be careful with yes/no oracles.
8. **"What do I do with the voice of my own shame, or the voice of my abuser, when I get quiet?"** For some, quiet is not safe. The breath and silence you offer can bring the loud things up. (You address this partly in the Safety section, but the cards don't.)
9. **"Isn't it presumptuous to put words in God's mouth?"** The app never writes them; but the *person* does. A short, honest "what you wrote is your attempt to listen, it is not God's dictation" would calm Catholic, Orthodox and Reformed readers. Your Language rules say something like this. The cards don't.
10. **"Catholic/Orthodox: Where does the Church's teaching authority come in? What about my confessor or spiritual director?"** The cards say "trusted believer, pastor, mature friend." A Catholic reader needs the word *spiritual director* or *priest*, and an Orthodox reader needs *spiritual father*. Cheap to add, large in goodwill.
11. **"Does this require me to picture Jesus? I'm afraid of mental images / I can't see anything."** Aphantasia and trauma-related avoidance of images are real. You say "remember, don't picture," which is good; one card could say it directly.
12. **"I'm tired. I'm busy. I'm angry at God."** Anger and lament. A very large share of people who stop praying stopped because of that. "Is it for me?" has no card for the angry. The Psalms are full of them.

### 4. Order and grouping: would it calm or alarm a first-time reader?

- **Calm: the toggle opening on "What's in the way?" is wise,** as you reason, because doubt comes before reasons.
- **Alarm: "Is it real?" is first, and it is the most argumentative group.** Someone who opens it nervously meets Hebrews 1, prophecy ceasing, and 1 Corinthians 13 within three cards. That is a lecture hall, not a living room. I'd resolve your open design question this way: **"Is it safe?" first.** Safety is the first worry for most people, and the sentence "you can stop any time, no one tells you what God said" is the one that lets them breathe. Put "I've been hurt," "What if I'm making it up?" and "What if it isn't God?" at the top, with "I'm not sure God is there" right after them.
- **Alarm: card titles that name danger first.** "What if I hear something frightening" and "Isn't this New Age" are in the same screen as the gentle ones. For a nervous person, the *list of titles* is itself content: it tells them what other people have feared. Keep them, but consider ordering from small fears to bigger ones so the first thing they see is "I feel silly."
- **Calm:** "Is it for me?" and "What if it doesn't work?" are gently placed and gently written. I would move "I tried and nothing happened" earlier too. It is the most common thing a real person will bring.
- **The Not sure? sheet.** A person who taps it in the middle of a step lands on a card that may contain a debate. For that route, the *safe* and *silence* cards should be the ones suggested; the apologetics cards (prophecy ceasing, Hebrews 1) should never appear as an interruption in the middle of a prayer. They belong in the browse view only.

### 5. Where the app argues and a pastor would simply sit with the person

- **"Isn't the Bible enough?"**: The first two sentences are the pastor's answer ("The Bible is the standard. Nothing in this app adds to it."). Stop there and let the Why section carry the rest. A pastor would also ask "what happened that made you ask?" The card can't, but it can say "Many people ask this because they love Scripture and are afraid of losing it."
- **"Doesn't prophecy cease?"**: The card says "We think Scripture says otherwise," then explains. A pastor would say "Faithful Christians have read this verse differently for two thousand years, and you may stay where you are." You say that once. Good. But "This is a serious question, so The Case takes it step by step" sounds like a debater lining up the next move. Remove it.
- **"Isn't hearing from God just subjective?"**: The tone is philosophical ("posture, authority"). A person asking this might be burned, or might be defending their faith. Lead with "Yes, in one sense, and that's why we test," then the Scripture point.
- **"My church doesn't teach this."**: Drop the closing question about Jesus meaning John 10. Say "Your church has loved you and taught you, and you don't have to choose."
- **"What if I'm just making it up?"**: Explain less. "It may be your own thoughts. That's okay. Write it down and bring it to someone you trust" is enough.
- **"Is Jesus in the room?"**: The answer is theology-by-bullet. A pastor would say "He promised to be with you. You can ask Him to be near and trust that He is."
- **Last line of The Case, "forged banknotes."** Nice line, but it's a debating point. Not in these cards, so no change; I mention it because the Not sure? sheet might link there.

### 6. Questions

**To the author**

1. Who is the *first* reader in your head: the doubter, the fragile, or the curious believer? The cards currently try to serve all three and the tone drifts between them.
2. What does the app do when someone writes something concerning in a note? The Safety section has a policy for the Healing path. Does it apply on the Objections cards?
3. Have you read these cards to anyone who has been through spiritual abuse, and watched their face? Not asked their opinion; watched.
4. Are you ready to cut a card if a pastor says it isn't ready? "I don't have time" and "Frightening or wrong" are the likeliest.
5. Who is your named pastoral reviewer in each tradition, and who is the clinical reviewer? "Pastors from more than one tradition" is still a plan, not a person.
6. What would you want the app to say to someone who finishes every card, tries, and hears nothing for a year?
7. The app is an aid for prayer. Where is the sentence, once and early, that says *you need a church, a pastor, a friend, and perhaps a counselor, and this does not replace any of them*?

**To a Reformed colleague**
- Does "The difference is authority, not posture" adequately keep Scripture as the sole infallible rule? Would you want "illumination" named in the subjective card?
- Is it acceptable that individual impressions are "tested" yet never called "revelation"? Does the app's word choice ("hear," "ask") invite that language?
- Would you be comfortable with a member of your church using this under the oversight of the elders?

**To an evangelical or charismatic colleague**
- Is the testing framework too cautious, or does it protect people the way you want?
- Does the app too quickly assume that hearing is a normal and universal expectation, in a way that could make some believers feel they are behind? ("Hearing is assumed, commanded and trained" in The Case has real force, and I worry it undermines the promise "it doesn't mean something is wrong with you.")
- Would you add a place for corporate discernment (1 Cor 14:29), since the app is all personal?

**To a Catholic or Orthodox colleague**
- Where does spiritual direction, confession, or the spiritual father belong in the "who do I test it with" language?
- Is the Church's discernment tradition (Ignatian discernment of spirits, the Philokalia's caution about "visions") something the app should nod to, rather than only "test it against Scripture"?
- Is "Jesus in the room" language safe from the Orthodox perspective, and does the app handle Real Presence respectfully by not touching it?
- What about the saints and Mary? The cards say nothing. Silence is good, but would you want a line that says "this app doesn't take a position on that"?

**To a Baptist colleague**
- Is "soul competency" and the priesthood of the believer respected, or does the app too lightly send people to pastors?
- Does the card on "My church doesn't teach this" respect local-church autonomy and the pastor's role?
- Does "the Holy Spirit speaks" language run into cessationist convictions in your congregation, and how would you want it phrased for them?

**To a clinician or a pastoral counselor**
- Rewrite the "frightening or wrong" card with me. What are the plain-language signs that mean "see someone today"?
- Is the breath-and-silence practice safe for a person with trauma, or should the first screen mention that closing eyes is optional?

### 7. What I am confident of, and what I want a real colleague to confirm

**Confident of:**
- The cards where the weight is comfort (not sure God is there, not good enough, silly, nothing came) are sound in substance and tone. Small wording changes only.
- The feeling tests (peace vs. condemnation) as written can harm scrupulous and depressed readers, and the "fades over time" claim is not true. This is not a matter of tradition.
- The 988 line belongs where your Safety section puts it.
- The citation problems I listed are real: I read Hebrews 8:8–12, Romans 8:14–16, 1 Cor 13:8–12, Colossians 2:8–10, Matthew 23:1–4, 1 Sam 3:1–10, 1 John 4:1–3, 18, Psalm 62:1–2, 1 Kings 19:11–13, Habakkuk 2:1–3, John 14:23, Proverbs 11:14.
- The missing objections (grief, mental illness and medication, big decisions, someone else's word about me) are real, from my experience of how these conversations go.

**Not confident of, and want a real colleague to confirm:**
- Whether each tradition's one-line reaction below matches what a pastor from that tradition would actually say. I'm an AI writing in a pastoral voice. I know the shape of these positions but not each individual's.
- The clinical phrasing on voices, psychosis, and mania. I'm not a clinician and neither of us should settle it alone.
- The Catholic and Orthodox points, especially on spiritual direction and the discernment of spirits. Ask someone inside each tradition.
- Whether I have the right balance on conviction versus condemnation. Pastors differ on this, and it deserves a real conversation.
- Several passages I did not re-read in context this time (Luke 15:20, Romans 5:8, Matthew 7:9–11, Romans 8:1, Galatians 5:22–23, Ezra 1:5 and the others in the Reach of Faith page). I know them well, and I think the uses are fair, but a colleague should check them.

Grace and peace,
A pastor who would rather you ship it slowly

# Card-by-card review

Format for each card: **Read** (what it does to the person), **Scripture** (does it carry the weight), **Traditions** (R = Reformed, E/C = evangelical/charismatic, Cath/Orth = Catholic/Orthodox, B = Baptist), **Verdict**, and a suggested rewording where I mark ADJUST or HOLD.

## Group 1: Is it real?

### 1.1 "Does God actually speak to ordinary people today?" — ADJUST

- **Read:** Good and short. "Some faithful Christians believe He stopped speaking beyond Scripture. We think Scripture itself says otherwise" is honest, but it's the first card, and it starts with a disagreement.
- **Scripture:** John 10:27 and 14:26 are on point (Jesus to His disciples, and the promise about the Spirit teaching and reminding, which in context was first promised to the apostles). Hebrews 8:11 is about knowing the Lord, not about hearing guidance. Replace with Romans 8:14 or John 16:13, or cite it as "all will know Him."
- **Traditions:** R: wants John 14:26 flagged as first spoken to the apostles; E/C: blesses; Cath/Orth: blesses, wants the Church mentioned; B: blesses.
- **Rewording:** "Jesus said His sheep hear His voice, and He said it to ordinary people. He promised the Spirit would teach and remind. Christians have understood this in different ways, and you can hold it with them. This app is a way to ask and listen, always under Scripture, and always tested."

### 1.2 "Isn't the Bible enough?" — ADJUST

- **Read:** The first two sentences are right. "If something you hear disagrees with Scripture, it isn't from God" is a good, clear line.
- **Scripture:** 2 Tim 3:16–17 says Scripture equips for every good work. Reformed readers will say that is exactly the point. The card answers a claim it never states. Acts 17:11 is fine. John 10:27 repeated three times in a row across cards starts to sound like one verse carrying the whole app.
- **Traditions:** R: wants "sufficient" taken seriously and not rebutted; E/C: blesses; Cath/Orth: wants Scripture *within* the Church's reading; B: blesses.
- **Rewording:** "The Bible is God's word and the standard for everything. Nothing here adds to it. If something you hear disagrees with it, put it down. Many people ask this because they love Scripture and don't want to lose it. This app wants to help you listen under it, not around it."

### 1.3 "Doesn't Hebrews say God has spoken by His Son… Doesn't prophecy cease?" — ADJUST

- **Read:** Strong objection, stated fairly. But this is a debate card on the first screen of a nervous reader's journey.
- **Scripture:** Hebrews 1:1–2 and John 16:13–15 are fairly used. 1 Cor 13:8–12: the card says "face to face … hasn't happened yet," which is a respectable reading and the one most charismatic and many evangelical scholars hold. Reformed cessationists read "the perfect" differently (the completed canon). The card says "Paul explains that this means 'face to face'" which states the reading as if Paul had said "the perfect = face to face." Soften.
- **Traditions:** R: will object (cessationist) or accept (continuationist); E/C: blesses; Cath/Orth: wince at the text-argument approach but agree on the conclusion; B: mixed, many are cessationist.
- **Rewording:** "Hebrews says God has spoken finally in His Son, and nothing here adds to Him. Christians have long read 1 Corinthians 13 in different ways. Some believe prophecy ended with the apostles, and some believe it continues until we see Him face to face. You can stay where you are. If you want to see why this app leans the second way, the Case lays it out." Mark it **browse view only**, never in the Not sure? sheet.

### 1.4 "My church doesn't teach this." — ADJUST

- **Read:** Kind opening ("That's okay, and many good churches don't emphasize it"). The closing sentence ("It asks whether Jesus meant what He said in John 10") is a nudge against the person's church.
- **Scripture:** John 10:27 fine; Proverbs 11:14 is about counselors, apt.
- **Traditions:** R/B: object to the closing line; E/C: fine; Cath/Orth: fine and want the priest named.
- **Rewording:** "That's okay. Many good churches teach this differently, and nothing here asks you to leave yours or take on a label. If you try it, bring what you write to your pastor, priest, or someone mature in your church, and let them help you weigh it."

### 1.5 "I'm not sure God is there. Is this for me?" — BLESS

- **Read:** This is the best card in the section. "No one is asking you to pretend" is exactly right.
- **Scripture:** Mark 9:24 is a father in desperate need speaking to Jesus and being helped. Matthew 7:7 fits.
- **Traditions:** All four bless it.
- One suggestion: end with "You can also just sit quietly and say nothing at all. That's a prayer too." No change required.

### 1.6 "Isn't hearing from God just subjective? At least Scripture is objective." — ADJUST

- **Read:** Intellectually good, but philosophical. A person asking this may be asking whether they can trust *themselves*.
- **Scripture:** John 5:39–40 and Luke 24:32 are legitimate. 1 Cor 2:12–14 is the right illumination text. "The difference is authority, not posture" is the line a Reformed pastor will want to check, since it could be read as flattening Scripture's objective character. Add a clause: "Scripture's meaning does not change; our reception of it does."
- **Traditions:** R: close, wants illumination named; E/C: blesses; Cath/Orth: blesses ("sacred Tradition" shaped); B: blesses.
- **Rewording:** "In one way, yes: what you hear is only as good as your listening, which is why we test it. Scripture is the measure, and its meaning doesn't change with our mood. But even reading Scripture as God's word takes a kind of trust. The leaders in John 5 knew every verse and still did not come to Him. We need the Spirit to open our eyes, even to the Bible. So we reach, and we test."

### 1.7 "Isn't this just for pastors or mystics?" — ADJUST

- **Read:** Warm and short. Good.
- **Scripture:** "Sheep, not staff" is lovely. Jeremiah 31:34 and Hebrews 8:11 are about knowing the Lord, not hearing. The claim "Hearing Him is part of being His" is a bit stronger than what these verses carry. Use John 10:3–5 only, or add Romans 8:14.
- **Traditions:** R: wince at "part of being His" if read as "every Christian will hear audibly"; E/C: blesses; Cath/Orth: blesses (the saints were ordinary); B: blesses.
- **Rewording:** "Jesus talked about His sheep, not His staff. The new covenant promise is that all of them will know Him, from the least to the greatest. Hearing Him grows with practice, like any friendship, and quiet seasons are part of that too."

## Group 2: Is it safe?

### 2.1 "What if I'm just making it up?" — ADJUST (important)

- **Read:** The first two sentences are kind. "A good word tends to hold up, and one that was only you tends to fade" is false (see the letter) and gives a person a false test. For a scrupulous person, "it may be only me" is the beginning of a spiral and the card adds a second test they can fail.
- **Scripture:** Romans 8:16 is about the Spirit bearing witness that we are God's children. It doesn't address discerning one's own thoughts. 1 Thess 5:21 is right (and its neighbor, "do not despise prophecies," v. 20, is worth showing).
- **Traditions:** R: wince at Romans 8:16; E/C: bless the gist; Cath/Orth: bless, want a director named; B: bless.
- **Rewording:** "It may be your own thoughts. Often that's how God speaks, through a mind He made. You don't have to be sure. Write it down. Ask whether it agrees with Scripture and points you to Jesus. Bring it to someone you trust. If it asks you to do something big or hard, wait. If you can't tell, you don't have to decide. Put it down and come back another day." Remove the "fades" line.
- Scripture to use: 1 Thess 5:21, 1 John 4:1; drop Romans 8:16 or move it to "Is it for me?"

### 2.2 "What if it isn't God, but something else?" — ADJUST (important)

- **Read:** The tests are largely sound (Scripture, Jesus, harm). The line "condemns you" and "fear, shame and pressure" has the problem I describe at length in the letter. "Not from God" is a flat verdict a fragile reader can't apply.
- **Scripture:** 1 John 4:1–3 tests by *confession of Jesus*, which is narrower than "fruit." Galatians 5:22–23 describes the fruit of a *life*, not of a single impression. Romans 8:1 is about status before God, not about feelings. "Will not follow a stranger" (John 10:5) is right.
- **Traditions:** R: wants Scripture first and Christ-confession (1 John 4); E/C: wants Spirit's conviction acknowledged; Cath/Orth: wants discernment of spirits tradition and a director; B: blesses.
- **Rewording:** "That's a fair worry. The Bible gives a plain test. Does it agree with Scripture? Does it honor Jesus? Does it ask you to hurt yourself or anyone, or to hide something from people who love you? If so, put it down and talk to someone you trust. If it only leaves you feeling heavy, that alone doesn't tell you who it's from. Sorrow over something you have really done wrong can be God's kindness, and a heavy mood can come from many things. When you can't tell, don't decide alone."

### 2.3 "Isn't this New Age or visualization?" — ADJUST

- **Read:** "Different in kind" is fair and your Language rules (remember, don't visualize) carry weight. "New Age practice seeks power or self-realization, or an impersonal energy" is a sweeping generalization, and a critic who knows the Immanuel literature will notice.
- **Scripture:** Colossians 2:8–10 warns against philosophies that take us captive. Fits loosely. 1 John 4:1 is the real anchor. John 14:6 is true, but the card's use of it implies that the key distinction is "in Jesus' name," which the app doesn't actually require.
- **Traditions:** R: wants clearer concern about imagination in worship (the second commandment tradition); E/C: fine; Cath/Orth: fine, and they have Ignatian imagination and icons, so they'll wonder why you're apologizing; B: fine.
- **Rewording:** "Some Christians have real concerns about methods that rely on imagination, and your pastor is a good person to talk with. This app doesn't ask you to picture anything. It asks you to remember, to ask, and to write what you notice. You are talking to a person, God, and you test what comes against His Word. You can skip any step."

### 2.4 "Is Jesus 'in the room'? He's in heaven." — ADJUST

- **Read:** Good question and a respectful answer.
- **Scripture:** Matthew 28:20 and John 14:23 are right. Hebrews 4:16 is about approaching the throne of grace, which supports "He is near" only indirectly. Consider John 14:18, Matthew 18:20, or Colossians 1:27.
- **Traditions:** R: fine if framed as presence by the Spirit; E/C: fine; Cath/Orth: fine, their theology of presence is rich; B: fine.
- **Rewording:** "Jesus is risen and at the Father's right hand. He also said, 'I am with you always,' and that He and the Father would come and make their home with the one who loves Him. This app doesn't ask you to summon Him or picture Him. You can simply welcome Him and ask Him to be near."

### 2.5 "What if I hear something frightening or wrong?" — HOLD

- **Read:** This is the most important card in the section and the one most likely to be read in the worst hour. As drafted, it combines four things (spiritual testing, harm, mental illness, crisis) into one paragraph and handles them unevenly.
- **Problems:**
  - "If it condemns you" (see letter).
  - "Voices that aren't like your own thoughts" as the sign of illness contradicts the other cards and doesn't match how people actually experience early psychosis, mania or severe depression.
  - The 988 line sits in the card, against your own Safety rule.
  - It doesn't mention medication, sleep, or the fact that a pastor should not be the only person told.
  - "Test it" is told to someone who may be frightened out of their wits.
- **Scripture:** Romans 8:1 and 1 John 4:1 are fine as comfort ("God is not the author of this fear," 1 Cor 14:33 is also a candidate) but not as diagnostic tools.
- **Traditions:** All four would want this written with a clinician. Cath/Orth especially: the discernment of spirits tradition is careful here and says "do not trust visions that terrify you; speak to your director."
- **Draft for a clinician and a pastor to improve:** "If what you hear or see frightens you, tells you to hurt yourself or anyone else, feels like it's watching or controlling you, or comes when you haven't slept or when your mind is racing: stop, and tell someone today. A doctor, a counselor, a pastor, a friend. Prayer is not a substitute for care, and you haven't done anything wrong. You can close the app. We'll be here when you're ready." The 988 line shows only after a worrisome input, not here.
- **Needs:** A clinician's read before use.

### 2.6 "I've been hurt by people who said they heard from God." — ADJUST

- **Read:** The heart is right. "We are sorry" is an app talking, though. Who is "we"? A survivor can tell. A plain "I'm sorry that happened" in the author's own voice (or "That should not have happened to you") is more believable.
- **Scripture:** Matthew 23:4 is about hypocritical teachers imposing burdens, an apt cousin but not a match for "claimed to hear from God." Better: Matthew 23:8–10 ("you have one Teacher"), or Ezekiel 34 (God against shepherds who harm the sheep).
- **Traditions:** All bless the tone. R: wants church discipline mentioned for real abuse; E/C: fine; Cath/Orth: fine, wants safe-environment resources; B: fine.
- **Rewording:** "I'm sorry. That should never have happened to you. Nobody has the right to tell you what God said. There's no leader here and nothing to join. You decide what to write and what to keep, and you can stop at any time. If you ever want to talk about what happened, a counselor who understands spiritual abuse can help." (Add that one line; it is where many survivors need the next step.)

## Group 3: Is it for me?

### 3.1 "I'm not good enough for Him to speak to me." — BLESS

- **Read:** Tender and true. The prodigal runs to meet the son before he finishes his speech. That is exactly the gospel.
- **Scripture:** Luke 15:20, Romans 5:8, Hebrews 4:16 all fit.
- **Traditions:** All bless it. A Catholic may want a sentence about confession for those weighed by real sin, but that is a separate card.
- Optional addition: "If you are carrying something specific, you can tell Him. He already knows."

### 3.2 "What if He tells me something I don't want to hear?" — ADJUST

- **Read:** The first sentence is good (good gifts, not stones). "Where there's correction it comes with love, and His way of correcting is not shame" is true and a relief to some. "If what you hear leaves you condemned, test it again" again risks the feelings-as-verdict problem, and nothing is said about *conviction being real*.
- **Scripture:** Matthew 7:9–11 fits. 1 John 4:18 ("perfect love casts out fear … fear has to do with punishment") is a true comfort for believers, but it can be turned into "if you're afraid, you aren't loved" by an anxious reader. Hebrews 12:5–11 (discipline from a loving Father) is a more honest companion.
- **Traditions:** R: wants the law/gospel distinction (conviction then comfort); E/C: fine; Cath/Orth: wants repentance named; B: fine.
- **Rewording:** "The Father gives good gifts, not stones. If He corrects you, it is the correction of a father who loves you. It is never contempt, and it doesn't leave you hopeless. It may sting, and it will always point you toward Him. If what you hear leaves you crushed with no way forward, don't carry it alone. Talk to someone you trust."

### 3.3 "I feel silly talking to the air." — BLESS

- **Read:** Lovely. "Write to God as you would a letter" is the right permission.
- **Scripture:** Samuel at the age of a boy did not yet know the Lord (1 Sam 3:7) and needed Eli's help. That is apt and fairly used.
- **Traditions:** All bless.

### 3.4 "I don't have time." — ADJUST (or cut)

- **Read:** Reads like a product FAQ. It doesn't carry a person.
- **Scripture:** None, which is fine, but it is the only card without a verse or a heart.
- **Traditions:** All indifferent.
- **Rewording or cut:** "Even a minute counts. You can come with one question and one breath. You can stop at any step and the app will keep where you were." If you can't give it warmth, drop it.

## Group 4: What if it doesn't work?

### 4.1 "I tried and nothing happened." — ADJUST

- **Read:** Warm; "doesn't mean something is wrong with you" is the sentence this card needs. But "Sometimes the answer is a quiet sense of peace, a word, a verse, or nothing yet" can feel like the app is quietly re-labeling silence as a result.
- **Scripture:** 1 Kings 19:11–13 fits. Habakkuk 2:1 is apt (watchpost, wait). Psalm 62:1 is about waiting in silence *because of trust*, not about God's silence. Consider the lament psalms (Ps 13, Ps 22:1–2, Ps 88) for the honest case where the person feels God is absent. Also note Elijah was exhausted and God *first fed him and let him sleep* (19:5–8).
- **Traditions:** R: fine; E/C: wants "keep persisting"; Cath/Orth: wants the dark night of the soul named, and will bless that; B: fine.
- **Rewording:** "That's common, and it doesn't mean something is wrong with you. Many faithful people have had long quiet seasons. Elijah, after his worst week, was first fed and allowed to sleep, and only then heard a whisper. Sometimes the kindest thing is rest. You can try the 'Nothing came?' page, or just thank Him for something and come back another day. If the silence hurts, tell Him that. The Psalms do."

### 4.2 "What if it's just my own thoughts?" — ADJUST

- **Read:** The line "Your own thoughts are still where God usually meets you" overclaims (breaks "may" and "often" rule). The card is also a duplicate of 2.1.
- **Scripture:** None cited. Fine.
- **Traditions:** R: wince at "usually"; E/C: bless; Cath/Orth: bless; B: fine.
- **Rewording:** Merge with 2.1, or: "See 'What if I'm making it up?' God may speak through your own mind. That's one way people have described it. You don't have to settle it. Write it down and let it be."

### 4.3 "What if I don't know what to do with what I hear?" — BLESS, with one addition

- **Read:** One of the wisest cards. "Hold it lightly. You don't have to act on anything right away. God is not in a hurry." That is exactly the pastor's voice.
- **Scripture:** Proverbs 11:14 (guidance and counselors) and 1 Thess 5:21 fit.
- **Traditions:** All bless.
- **Addition (I'd make it):** "If it touches your marriage, your children, your health or medication, a large sum of money, or a choice that can't be undone, don't act on it alone and don't act on it quickly. Ask the people who love you." That is the sentence I wish someone had said to three different members of my church.
- My verdict stays BLESS, because that addition is a gift to the card, not a repair.

## Standing check — ADJUST

1. **Does it fit Scripture?** Good.
2. **Does it point to Jesus rather than inflate me or demand certainty?** The phrase "demand certainty" is odd here; it's a different test. Split it: "Does it point to Jesus?" and "Does it make me proud, or demand that I be sure?"
3. **Does it carry peace, love and patience, or agitation, shame and striving?** This is the feelings test. Rewrite: "Does it lead toward love for God and for people? Peace can take time, and conviction can be real. If you can't tell, don't decide alone."
4. **Add a fourth:** "Have I told someone I trust?" It does more than the other three to keep people safe.

Reminder for placement: This check appears after every session and at the foot of the page. Presented as a three-question quiz at the end of a session, it can feel like a final exam. Frame it as "Three things to look at, when you're ready." A pastor offers a lamp, not a grade.
