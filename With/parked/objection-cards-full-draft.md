Status: parked on purpose. These are the cards written after the pastoral-frame review (see `../reviews/whats-in-the-way-pastoral-review.md`), before the section was cut back to the few that truly block believing God wants to talk with us.

Why parked: "What's in the way?" should only hold the barriers to *accepting that God wants to talk with us*. A long list of objections teaches people that there are many reasons to doubt. The rest of this material is still valuable, but it belongs where it is needed: in the Care page ("When it's heavy"), in quiet practice notes at the right step, or in More > Common questions. See DESIGN.md.

Where each card goes:
- Stays in What's in the way?: not sure God is there; not good enough; pastors or mystics; nothing happened; Bible enough (merged with "my church doesn't teach this"); how do I know it's Him (merged "making it up" and "isn't God").
- Care page ("When it's heavy"): frightening or wrong; mental illness and medication; quiet isn't safe; grief; hurt by leaders; someone else's word about me; angry at God and why not rescue (lament).
- Practice notes: big life decisions (Keep step); asking about decisions, lotteries and exams (Daily "before a decision" prompt); putting words in God's mouth (Keep note); picturing Jesus (a note on the remember steps).
- More > Common questions (browse only, not in the main flow): New Age or visualization; Jesus "in the room"; arrogance; prophecy ceases and Hebrews.
- Why practice? > The Case and The Reach of Faith: Bible enough, Hebrews, prophecy, subjective.

### What's in the way? Answering Objections

This is the content of the *What's in the way?* frame. The frame is the flow (the toggle side and the **Not sure?** sheet). **Answering Objections** is the section of cards inside it.

Revised after the pastoral-frame review (`reviews/whats-in-the-way-pastoral-review.md`). All wording below is **draft** and still needs real pastors and, for the safety-sensitive cards, a clinician. No card claims more than Scripture does.

**Card rules (from the review):**
- **Companion voice, not debate voice.** Cards sit with the person. Argument lives in *Why practice?*. A card may point there, never argue at length.
- **Content tests lead; feelings never decide alone.** Tests are: does it agree with Scripture, does it honor Jesus, does it ask me to hurt anyone or hide something. Heaviness or peace alone does not tell the person who a thing is from. Conviction over real wrong can be God's kindness, and a heavy mood can come from many things. When in doubt: **don't decide alone.**
- **No "it fades" test.** Persistence is not evidence of God. Cut everywhere.
- **"May" and "often," never "usually" or "always."**
- **Name the people to test with in each tradition's words:** pastor, priest, spiritual director, spiritual father, elder, or a mature friend.
- **988 never appears in a card.** It appears only after a worrisome input (see Safety).
- **Cards that are heavy get a plain exit:** "You can close the app."
- **Apologetics cards are browse-only.** The **Not sure?** sheet offers only the gentle cards (marked *sheet*).
- **Each card:** the question in the person's words, a short answer, verses that actually carry the point in context, and where it fits a **Try this** that goes to something small and safe, plus a link to the matching *Why practice?* piece.

**Group order (decided):** *Is it safe?* first, since safety is the first worry for most people. Then *Is it for me?*, then *What if it doesn't work?*, then *Is it real?* last, with its debate cards browse-only. Within each group, gentler cards come first.

**Shown once, early, in the first-launch note and at the foot of this area:** *"This is a prayer aid. You need a church, a pastor or priest, a friend, and sometimes a counselor or doctor. This doesn't replace any of them."*

#### Is it safe?

**"I've been hurt by people who said they heard from God."** *(sheet)*
I'm sorry. That should never have happened to you. Nobody has the right to tell you what God said. There's no leader here and nothing to join. You decide what to write and what to keep, and you can stop at any time. If you want to talk about what happened, a counselor who understands spiritual abuse can help.
*Matthew 23:8–10 (you have one Teacher), Ezekiel 34:1–6, 10.* [draft]

**"What if I'm just making it up?"** *(sheet)*
It may be your own thoughts. Often that's how God speaks, through a mind He made. You don't have to be sure. Write it down. Ask whether it agrees with Scripture and points you to Jesus. Bring it to someone you trust. If it asks you to do something big or hard, wait. If you can't tell, you don't have to decide. Put it down and come back another day.
*1 Thessalonians 5:20–21, 1 John 4:1.* **See:** The Reach of Faith. **Try this:** the Test step. [draft]

**"What if it isn't God, but something else?"** *(sheet)*
That's a fair worry, and the Bible gives a plain test. Does it agree with Scripture? Does it honor Jesus? Does it ask you to hurt yourself or anyone, or to hide something from people who love you? If so, put it down and talk to someone you trust. If it only leaves you feeling heavy, that alone doesn't tell you who it's from. Sorrow over something you have really done wrong can be God's kindness, and a heavy mood can come from many things. When you can't tell, don't decide alone.
*1 John 4:1–3, John 10:5, 2 Corinthians 7:10.* [draft]

**"I live with anxiety, depression, OCD, bipolar or trauma. Is this safe for me?"** *(sheet)* **HOLD: needs a clinician and a pastor.**
It can be, gently, and you don't have to do it alone. Tell your counselor or doctor you're trying it. Don't change or stop any medication because of something you think you heard. If quiet or testing makes your mind louder, or you find yourself going round and round asking whether it was God, stop, breathe, and close the app. Rest and care are not a lack of faith. Elijah was fed and slept before he heard anything.
*1 Kings 19:5–8.* [draft, hold]

**"What if I hear something frightening or wrong?"** *(sheet)* **HOLD: rewrite with a clinician and a pastor before use.**
If what you hear or see frightens you, tells you to hurt yourself or anyone else, feels like it's watching or controlling you, or comes when you haven't slept or when your mind is racing, stop, and tell someone today: a doctor, a counselor, a pastor, a friend. Prayer is not a substitute for care, and you haven't done anything wrong. You can close the app. We'll be here when you're ready.
*1 Corinthians 14:33 (God is not a God of confusion).* No 988 line here. [draft, hold]

**"When I get quiet, the loud things come: shame, an old voice, memories."** *(sheet)* **HOLD: clinician review.**
Then stillness isn't safe for you right now, and that isn't your failure. Keep your eyes open, keep a light on, keep it short. Use the glad place, or simply stop. Many people need a counselor to help them find quiet safely. You can come back when it's easier.
*Psalm 46:1, Matthew 11:28.* [draft, hold]

**"I'm grieving. Can I hear from the one I lost?"** **HOLD: pastoral and clinical review.**
I'm so sorry. Grief is welcome here, and you can bring all of it to God, including the wish for one more word from them. This app is for listening to God. Scripture asks us not to seek the dead for answers, and it also promises that God is near to the brokenhearted and holds those who have died in Christ. You don't have to be okay. If the weight is too heavy, a pastor, a counselor, or a friend can sit with you.
*Deuteronomy 18:10–12, Psalm 34:18, John 11:35, 1 Thessalonians 4:13–14.* [draft, hold]

**"Someone told me God said something about me."**
Weigh it. You don't have to obey it, or even accept it. A word from another person is something to test like any other: with Scripture, with prayer, and with people who know you. A word that needs you to hurry, to keep it secret, or to pull away from people who love you is a reason to slow down. You can say, "Thank you, I'll pray about it," and leave it there.
*1 Thessalonians 5:20–21, 1 Corinthians 14:29.* [draft]

**"Isn't this New Age or visualization?"**
Some Christians have real concerns about methods that rely on imagination, and your pastor, priest or spiritual director is a good person to talk with. This app doesn't ask you to picture anything. It asks you to remember, to ask, and to write what you notice. You are talking to a person, God, and you test what comes against His Word. You can skip any step.
*1 John 4:1.* [draft]

**"Is Jesus 'in the room'? He's in heaven."**
Jesus is risen and at the Father's right hand. He also said, "I am with you always," and that He and the Father would come and make their home with the one who loves Him. This app doesn't ask you to summon Him or picture Him. You can simply welcome Him and ask Him to be near.
*Matthew 28:20, John 14:18, John 14:23.* [draft; presence wording still open, gate 3]

#### Is it for me?

**"I feel silly talking to the air."** *(sheet)*
Many do at first. Samuel didn't yet know the Lord, and an older believer helped him. You can start by writing to God as you would a letter. Over time it becomes more natural.
*1 Samuel 3:1–10.* **Try this:** "Dear God," and write one line. [draft]

**"I'm not sure God is there. Is this for me?"** *(sheet)*
Yes. You don't need to be certain. A man once told Jesus, "I believe; help my unbelief," and Jesus helped him. You can treat this as asking honestly and seeing what happens. No one is asking you to pretend. You can also just sit quietly and say nothing at all. That's a prayer too.
*Mark 9:24, Matthew 7:7.* **Try this:** "God, if You're there, I'd like to know You." [draft]

**"I'm not good enough for Him to speak to me."** *(sheet)*
God's love isn't a reward for being good. The father in Jesus' story ran to meet a son who had wasted everything, before he had finished his speech. You are invited to come near. If you are carrying something specific, you can tell Him. He already knows.
*Luke 15:20, Romans 5:8, Hebrews 4:16.* [draft]

**"Isn't it arrogant to say God speaks to me?"** *(sheet)*
Many humble people feel this. Hearing isn't a sign that you are special. It's what sheep do, and sheep aren't remarkable. Holding what you hear loosely ("I think... I may be wrong") fits the posture. Listening is one of the humblest things a person can do.
*1 Samuel 3:9–10, Luke 1:38.* [draft]

**"What if He tells me something I don't want to hear?"**
The Father gives good gifts, not stones. If He corrects you, it is the correction of a father who loves you. It is never contempt, and it doesn't leave you hopeless. It may sting, and it will always point you toward Him. If what you hear leaves you crushed with no way forward, don't carry it alone. Talk to someone you trust.
*Matthew 7:9–11, Hebrews 12:5–11.* [draft]

**"Do I have to picture Jesus? I can't see anything."** *(sheet)*
No. You never have to picture anything. Remembering, noticing, or simply asking is enough. Some people see nothing at all, and some find images upsetting. Many people hear through a thought, a verse, or a calm. Skip any step that asks for more than you want to give.
*John 20:29.* **See:** How He speaks. [draft]

**"Isn't it presumptuous to put words in God's mouth?"**
It would be, and the app never does. What you write is your attempt to listen. It is not God's dictation. Treat it as a note of what you noticed, hold it loosely, and test it.
*Proverbs 30:5–6, Deuteronomy 18:20.* [draft]

**"I don't have time."**
Even a minute counts. You can come with one question and one breath. You can stop at any step, and the app will keep where you were. [draft; cut if it can't be given warmth]

#### What if it doesn't work?

**"I tried and nothing happened."** *(sheet)*
That's common, and it doesn't mean something is wrong with you. Many faithful people have had long quiet seasons. Elijah, after his worst week, was first fed and allowed to sleep, and only then heard a whisper. Sometimes the kindest thing is rest. You can try the "Nothing came?" page, or just thank Him for something and come back another day. If the silence hurts, tell Him that. The Psalms do.
*1 Kings 19:5–13, Psalm 13:1–2, Habakkuk 2:1.* **Try this:** "Nothing came?" [draft]

**"What if I don't know what to do with what I hear?"** *(sheet)*
Hold it lightly. Write it down, bring it to Scripture, and bring it to someone mature in the faith. You don't have to act on anything right away. God is not in a hurry. If it touches your marriage, your children, your health or medication, a large sum of money, or a choice that can't be undone, don't act on it alone and don't act on it quickly. Ask the people who love you.
*Proverbs 11:14, 1 Thessalonians 5:21.* [draft]

**"What if I think God is telling me to leave my spouse, quit my job, confront someone, or give a lot of money?"** *(sheet)* **HOLD: pastoral review.**
Slow down. The big ones are the places where a "word" can do the most harm: your marriage, your children, your health or medication, a large sum of money, confronting someone, a call to ministry, anything that can't be undone. Don't act on it alone, and don't act on it quickly. Take it to people who love you and know you, such as a pastor, priest, spiritual director, a mature friend, or a counselor, and let them help you weigh it. A real word from God can wait, and it will still be true next week.
*Proverbs 11:14, Proverbs 15:22, 1 Thessalonians 5:21.* [draft, hold]

**"Can I ask Him what to do? Whether someone loves me? What's on the exam?"**
Ask for wisdom and for His presence. Be careful with yes-or-no questions about the future or about what is in another person's heart, since that road drifts toward guessing games. A better question is, "What do You want me to know about this? What is the next faithful step?" For big choices, bring the people who love you in.
*James 1:5, Proverbs 3:5–6.* [draft]

**"Why would He speak to me but not rescue my child, or stop what happened to me?"** **HOLD: pastoral review.**
I don't know, and I won't pretend to. Many people ask this, including in the Bible: "How long, O Lord?" Silence and pain can feel like the same abandonment. You don't have to make peace with it today. You can tell Him exactly what you think. If you want a person to sit with you in this, a pastor or counselor can.
*Psalm 13:1–2, Psalm 22:1–2, John 11:21, 35.* [draft, hold]

**"I'm angry at God."** *(sheet)*
Then tell Him. The Psalms are full of honest anger, and He kept them in the Bible. Anger told to God is still talking to God. You don't have to be polite, and you don't have to hear anything back today.
*Psalm 13:1–2, Psalm 62:8.* [draft]

#### Is it real? *(debate cards: browse-only, never in the Not sure? sheet)*

**"Does God actually speak to ordinary people today?"**
Jesus said His sheep hear His voice, and He said it to ordinary people. He promised the Spirit would teach and remind. Christians have understood this in different ways, and you can hold it with them. This app is a way to ask and listen, always under Scripture, and always tested.
*John 10:27, John 14:26, Romans 8:14.* **See:** The Case. [draft]

**"Isn't the Bible enough?"**
The Bible is God's word and the standard for everything. Nothing here adds to it. If something you hear disagrees with it, put it down. Many people ask this because they love Scripture and don't want to lose it. This app wants to help you listen under it, not around it.
*Acts 17:11, John 5:39–40.* **See:** The Reach of Faith. [draft]

**"Doesn't Hebrews say God has spoken by His Son? Doesn't prophecy cease?"**
Hebrews says God has spoken finally in His Son, and nothing here adds to Him. Christians have long read 1 Corinthians 13 in different ways. Some believe prophecy ended with the apostles, and some believe it continues until we see Him face to face. You can stay where you are. If you want to see why this app leans the second way, The Case lays it out.
*Hebrews 1:1–2, John 16:13–15, 1 Corinthians 13:8–12.* **See:** The Case, step 8. [draft]

**"My church doesn't teach this."**
That's okay. Many good churches teach this differently, and nothing here asks you to leave yours or take on a label. If you try it, bring what you write to your pastor, priest, spiritual director, or someone mature in your church, and let them help you weigh it.
*Proverbs 11:14.* [draft]

**"Isn't hearing from God just subjective? At least Scripture is objective."**
In one way, yes. What you hear is only as good as your listening, which is why we test it. Scripture is the measure, and its meaning doesn't change with our mood. But even reading Scripture as God's word takes a kind of trust. The leaders in John 5 knew every verse and still did not come to Him. We need the Spirit to open our eyes, even to the Bible. So we reach, and we test.
*John 5:39–40, Luke 24:32, 1 Corinthians 2:12–14.* **See:** The Reach of Faith. [draft]

**"Isn't this just for pastors or mystics?"**
Jesus talked about His sheep, not His staff. The new covenant promise is that all of them will know Him, from the least to the greatest. Hearing Him grows with practice, like any friendship, and quiet seasons are part of that too.
*John 10:3–5, Romans 8:14, Jeremiah 31:34 (knowing Him).* [draft]

#### Standing check

Offered as **"Three things to look at, when you're ready,"** after a session and at the foot of this area. A lamp, not a grade.

1. **Does it agree with Scripture?**
2. **Does it point to Jesus?** And does it make me proud, or demand that I be sure?
3. **Does it lead toward love for God and for people?** Peace can take time, and conviction can be real. If I can't tell, I don't decide alone.
4. **Have I told someone I trust?** This one does more than the other three to keep people safe.
